## 1. Introduction

Sekolah Rendah Sathya Sai (SRSS) is committed to providing a safe, nurturing, respectful, and inclusive educational environment in which every child is valued and protected. The School recognises that the wellbeing and dignity of children must remain central to every educational activity, including the use of digital technologies.

The SRSS Digital Hub is established to support the School’s educational mission while progressively introducing digital services for pupils, parents, guardians, teachers, and authorised personnel.

This Child Protection & Online Safeguarding Policy establishes the principles and responsibilities governing the protection of children within the Platform. It addresses risks associated with online communications, inappropriate content, cyberbullying, privacy violations, unauthorised contact, misuse of personal information, and other forms of digital harm.

The Policy applies to all individuals who access, administer, contribute to, or interact with pupil-related services through the Platform.

The School’s safeguarding approach is guided by applicable Malaysian law, relevant educational requirements, and the universal Human Values of Truth, Right Conduct, Peace, Love, and Nonviolence.

The safety and best interests of children shall take precedence over convenience, publicity, administrative efficiency, or technological experimentation.

## 2. Institutional Commitment

SRSS recognises that children require special protection because of their age, developing maturity, and potential vulnerability to exploitation or harm. The School is committed to:

1. Protecting children from abuse, exploitation, harassment, and inappropriate contact.
2. Promoting safe and responsible digital participation.
3. Respecting children’s dignity and privacy.
4. Maintaining appropriate professional boundaries.
5. Establishing accessible reporting procedures.
6. Responding promptly to safeguarding concerns.
7. Restricting access to sensitive pupil information.
8. Providing age-appropriate guidance.
9. Supporting parents and guardians in promoting online safety.
10. Reviewing safeguarding practices as the Platform develops.

Safeguarding responsibilities extend beyond the technical security of the Platform. They include the behaviour of users, the suitability of digital interactions, the handling of information, and the School’s response to concerns.

## 3. Legal and Educational Framework

This Policy is intended to operate consistently with applicable Malaysian laws and relevant regulatory requirements. These may include:

- The Child Act 2001 (Act 611).
- The Education Act 1996 (Act 550).
- The Sexual Offences Against Children Act 2017 (Act 792).
- The Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 (Act 588).
- The Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Act 709), as amended, where applicable.
- Other relevant legislation, regulations, and Ministry of Education requirements.

The School shall also consider applicable guidance concerning child welfare, educational supervision, and the responsible use of digital technologies.

Nothing in this Policy limits statutory duties to report suspected abuse or other matters to the appropriate authorities. This Policy applies the School-wide [Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy](/legal/child-protection) to digital services; where the two overlap, the School-wide policy’s procedures apply.

## 4. Scope of the Policy

This Policy applies to digital activities conducted through or directly associated with the SRSS Digital Hub. These may include:

- Student accounts.
- Parent and guardian accounts.
- Teacher and staff accounts.
- Digital classrooms.
- Online assignments.
- Student portfolios.
- School communications.
- Digital forms.
- Educational recordings.
- Online meetings.
- School-managed cloud storage.
- Photographs and videos.
- School events and activities.
- Integrated third-party educational services.

The Policy also applies to authorised individuals who access pupil information for technical, administrative, educational, or governance purposes.

## 5. Safeguarding Governance and Responsibilities

### 5.1 Principal

The Principal serves as the designated operational person-in-charge for the SRSS Digital Hub. The Principal is responsible for ensuring that appropriate school procedures are established for Platform administration and safeguarding. These responsibilities include:

- Coordinating safeguarding responses.
- Authorising appropriate access arrangements.
- Ensuring concerns are referred to designated personnel.
- Supporting relevant staff training.
- Escalating serious matters to appropriate authorities.
- Reviewing significant incidents.
- Maintaining appropriate confidentiality.

The Principal may delegate specific operational responsibilities to suitably authorised personnel. Delegation does not remove institutional responsibilities established by applicable law.

### 5.2 Designated Safeguarding Personnel

The School’s Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL) is its Senior Assistant for Administration / Academic, supported by two Deputy Designated Safeguarding Leads: the School Counsellor and the Senior Assistant for Co-curriculum.

The Designated Safeguarding Lead coordinates reports, maintains appropriate records, and ensures that concerns are addressed through established procedures. Their full responsibilities, and the names and contact details of the current post-holders, are set out in the [Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy](/legal/child-protection#s-4).

### 5.3 Teachers and School Staff

Teachers and staff must:

- Maintain professional boundaries.
- Use approved communication channels.
- Protect pupil information.
- Supervise relevant digital activities.
- Report suspected safeguarding concerns.
- Avoid inappropriate private communications.
- Refrain from sharing unauthorised pupil media.
- Follow school procedures for incidents and disclosures.

### 5.4 Parents and Guardians

Parents and guardians are encouraged to support safe digital participation by:

- Supervising children’s online activities.
- Maintaining secure account credentials.
- Discussing respectful online behaviour.
- Reporting suspicious communications.
- Monitoring age-appropriate technology use.
- Respecting the privacy of other pupils.

### 5.5 Technical Administrators

Technical administrators must comply with confidentiality, access control, and safeguarding requirements. Technical privileges must not be used to inspect pupil information without legitimate authorisation.

Where access to sensitive records is necessary for troubleshooting, it should be limited, documented, and appropriately supervised.

## 6. Age-Appropriate Digital Access

SRSS serves primary-school pupils. Accordingly, digital services must be designed and configured with consideration for children’s age, maturity, and educational needs. The School may implement:

- Restricted communication functions.
- Limited public posting.
- Controlled external sharing.
- Teacher-supervised activities.
- Approved educational applications.
- Age-appropriate content.
- Restricted account permissions.
- Moderated student submissions.
- Additional safeguards for younger pupils.

Not all services available to adult users will be available to pupils. The School may adjust restrictions according to educational requirements and safeguarding considerations.

## 7. Appropriate Communication with Pupils

Communication between adults and pupils must remain professional, appropriate, and connected to legitimate educational purposes.

Teachers and authorised staff should use school-approved communication systems. Where practical, communications should be conducted through channels that provide appropriate institutional oversight.

Staff must not use school-related digital access to initiate inappropriate personal relationships with pupils.

### 7.1 Prohibited Communications

The following conduct is prohibited:

- Sexualised or suggestive communications.
- Requests for inappropriate photographs or recordings.
- Grooming or attempts to establish exploitative relationships.
- Threatening or intimidating messages.
- Requests for secrecy concerning inappropriate interactions.
- Unauthorised personal contact unrelated to legitimate school activities.
- Sharing harmful or age-inappropriate content.

Suspected violations must be reported promptly.

### 7.2 Parent-Teacher Communications

Communications concerning a child’s education should ordinarily take place through approved school channels. Teachers should avoid unnecessarily disclosing confidential information concerning other pupils.

### 7.3 Student-to-Student Communications

Where student messaging or collaboration is enabled, the School may impose restrictions and moderation arrangements.

Pupils must communicate respectfully and must not engage in bullying, harassment, impersonation, or unauthorised sharing of personal information.

## 8. Cyberbullying and Online Harassment

Cyberbullying includes the use of digital technologies to intimidate, humiliate, threaten, exclude, or repeatedly harm another person. Examples may include:

- Abusive messages.
- Repeated harassment.
- Deliberate humiliation.
- Spreading harmful rumours.
- Sharing embarrassing images.
- Impersonating another pupil.
- Excluding or targeting individuals through digital groups.
- Threatening communications.
- Unauthorised disclosure of personal information.

The School does not tolerate cyberbullying through its Platform. Reports should be assessed promptly and handled with sensitivity to the age and circumstances of the children involved.

Appropriate responses may include:

- Immediate protective measures.
- Parent or guardian notification where appropriate.
- Educational intervention.
- Restorative practices where suitable.
- Restrictions on digital access.
- Disciplinary procedures.
- Referral to appropriate authorities where necessary.

Responses should prioritise pupil safety, fairness, and the prevention of further harm.

## 9. Protection of Children’s Personal Information

Children’s personal information must be handled with particular care. The School shall endeavour to limit the collection, use, and disclosure of pupil information to legitimate educational and administrative purposes.

Sensitive information must not be made available through general-access directories, public pages, or unrestricted communication channels. Access to individual pupil records should be limited to authorised personnel and verified parents or guardians.

### 9.1 Pupil Identification

The School should avoid unnecessary publication of:

- Full dates of birth.
- Identification numbers.
- Residential addresses.
- Personal contact information.
- Medical conditions.
- Family circumstances.
- Custody information.
- Other sensitive identifying details.

### 9.2 Student Profiles

Student profiles are intended primarily for authorised educational use. Public access to detailed pupil profiles must not be enabled.

### 9.3 Educational Support Information

Records relating to learning difficulties, disabilities, counselling, safeguarding, or other sensitive matters require enhanced confidentiality. Such information must not be disclosed to unauthorised users.

## 10. Photography, Videography, and Media Publication

Photographs and recordings may support legitimate educational activities and the celebration of school achievements. However, the publication of children’s images requires careful consideration. The School shall distinguish between:

1. Internal educational documentation.
2. Restricted family access.
3. School-community publications.
4. Public website publication.
5. External promotional or social media publication.

Where legally required, appropriate parental or guardian consent must be obtained. Consent for one category of use does not automatically authorise all other categories.

The School should avoid publishing images or accompanying information that could unnecessarily expose children to identification, tracking, harassment, or other risks. Full provisions are set out in the [Photography, Video, Student Work & Media Consent Policy](/legal/media-consent).

### 10.1 Sensitive Situations

Photographs or recordings involving medical treatment, distress, disciplinary incidents, safeguarding concerns, or other sensitive circumstances must not be published for promotional purposes.

### 10.2 Student Work

Student-created materials may be displayed internally for educational purposes. Public publication must comply with applicable intellectual property, privacy, and consent requirements.

### 10.3 Withdrawal of Media Consent

Parents or guardians may request withdrawal of applicable media consent. The School shall take reasonable steps to cease future use and remove material from channels under its control where required and practicable.

The School cannot guarantee removal of copies independently retained or redistributed by third parties, but shall take appropriate steps within its lawful authority.

## 11. Online Meetings and Virtual Learning

Where online meetings or virtual learning sessions are conducted, the School shall endeavour to implement appropriate safeguards. These may include:

- Approved meeting platforms.
- Restricted meeting access.
- Appropriate teacher supervision.
- Controlled participant permissions.
- Suitable meeting settings.
- Restrictions on unauthorised recording.
- Clear rules concerning respectful behaviour.

Pupils should not be required to disclose unnecessary personal information during online sessions.

Recording of sessions involving children should occur only where justified and appropriately authorised. Recorded materials must be stored securely and retained only for legitimate purposes.

## 12. Third-Party Educational Applications

The SRSS Digital Hub may integrate with Google Workspace for Education and other approved services. The School shall endeavour to assess third-party applications before authorising their use by pupils. Relevant considerations include:

- Educational necessity.
- Age suitability.
- Privacy practices.
- Data security.
- Communication features.
- External sharing capabilities.
- Advertising or profiling practices.
- Data retention arrangements.
- Available parental controls.

Teachers and staff must not independently introduce applications requiring extensive pupil information without appropriate school authorisation.

Any individual who becomes aware of a potential safeguarding concern should report it promptly. Concerns may include:

- Suspected online grooming.
- Inappropriate adult-to-child communication.
- Cyberbullying.
- Threats or harassment.
- Unauthorised publication of pupil images.
- Exposure to harmful content.
- Suspicious account activity.
- Disclosure of sensitive information.
- Other conduct potentially endangering a child.

### 13.2 Confidentiality

Safeguarding reports must be handled sensitively. Information should be shared only with individuals who have a legitimate need to know or where disclosure is legally required.

Absolute confidentiality cannot be promised where disclosure is necessary to protect a child or comply with the law.

### 13.3 Protection Against Retaliation

Individuals who raise safeguarding concerns in good faith should not be subjected to retaliation for making a report.

Knowingly false or malicious reports may be addressed through appropriate procedures, without discouraging genuine disclosures.

## 14. Responding to Safeguarding Incidents

When a safeguarding concern is reported, the School should follow an appropriate response process. This may involve:

#### Step 1 — Receive and Record

Document the concern accurately, including relevant dates, circumstances, and available evidence.

#### Step 2 — Assess Immediate Safety

Determine whether urgent protective action is required.

#### Step 3 — Restrict Further Harm

Where appropriate, restrict relevant accounts, communications, or access privileges.

#### Step 4 — Escalate

Refer the matter to the Designated Safeguarding Lead, the Principal, or the appropriate authority.

#### Step 5 — Preserve Evidence

Preserve relevant information lawfully and securely. Staff should not conduct unnecessary or intrusive investigations that could compromise a child’s welfare or an official investigation.

#### Step 6 — Support the Child

Provide appropriate support and ensure that the child is not subjected to unnecessary distress or repeated questioning.

#### Step 7 — Review and Follow Up

Document appropriate outcomes, protective measures, and any necessary improvements to school procedures.

### 14.1 Allegations Involving School Personnel

Where a concern involves a teacher, employee, administrator, volunteer, or other adult associated with the School, the matter must be handled through an appropriately independent escalation process. A person who is the subject of an allegation must not control the investigation or determine access to relevant evidence.

Where the allegation concerns the Principal, it should be reported directly to the Chair of the Board of Governors of Sekolah Rendah Sathya Berhad, without first notifying the Principal, and, where required, to the relevant authorities.

## 15. Parental Notification

Parents or guardians should ordinarily be informed of safeguarding concerns affecting their children where appropriate. However, notification must be assessed carefully where it could:

- Increase the risk of harm.
- Compromise an investigation.
- Conflict with lawful directions.
- Expose a child to retaliation.
- Undermine necessary protective measures.

Decisions concerning notification should be made by appropriately authorised personnel in accordance with applicable safeguarding requirements.

## 16. Safeguarding Records

Safeguarding records must be maintained securely and separately from general educational information where appropriate. Such records may include:

- Incident reports.
- Relevant communications.
- Protective actions.
- Referral information.
- Follow-up records.
- Decisions and their rationale.

Access must be strictly limited. Records should not be included in ordinary student portfolios or general-access dashboards.

Retention and disposal must comply with applicable legal requirements and approved school procedures.

## 17. Responsibilities of Pupils

Pupils are encouraged to use the Platform responsibly and respectfully. Age-appropriate guidance should explain that pupils must:

- Treat others kindly.
- Keep passwords private.
- Avoid sharing personal information unnecessarily.
- Inform a trusted adult about upsetting or suspicious communications.
- Refrain from bullying.
- Respect other pupils’ photographs and work.
- Use approved educational services.
- Ask for help when uncertain.

Children should be encouraged to report concerns without fear of humiliation or inappropriate punishment.

The School recognises that younger pupils may make mistakes while learning to use digital technologies. Responses should therefore consider age, maturity, circumstances, and educational needs.

## 18. Responsibilities of Parents and Guardians

Parents and guardians are important partners in maintaining children’s online safety. They are encouraged to:

- Discuss appropriate online behaviour.
- Monitor age-appropriate technology use.
- Help children recognise suspicious communications.
- Maintain secure account credentials.
- Report concerns promptly.
- Respect school communication procedures.
- Avoid sharing images of other pupils without appropriate permission.
- Support the School’s safeguarding initiatives.

Parents should not attempt to independently investigate serious allegations involving children in ways that could compromise evidence, expose confidential information, or cause further harm.

## 19. Responsibilities of Staff and Volunteers

Staff and volunteers must maintain appropriate professional conduct when using digital technologies. They must not:

- Use unauthorised accounts to communicate privately with pupils.
- Share confidential pupil information.
- Publish pupil photographs without appropriate authorisation.
- Access records unrelated to their duties.
- Use school systems for inappropriate personal purposes.
- Ignore suspected safeguarding concerns.
- Retaliate against individuals who report concerns.

Relevant personnel should receive appropriate safeguarding guidance and training. Access to pupil-related systems should be reviewed when duties change or institutional involvement ends.

## 20. Technical Safeguards

The School shall endeavour to implement reasonable technical safeguards appropriate to the Platform’s development stage. These may include:

- Role-based permissions.
- Restricted student accounts.
- Controlled communication features.
- Secure authentication.
- Access logging.
- Administrative account controls.
- Restricted media sharing.
- Secure document storage.
- Periodic access reviews.
- Security updates.
- Incident reporting mechanisms.

Technical safeguards complement, but do not replace, appropriate supervision, professional conduct, and institutional safeguarding procedures.

## 21. Educational Approach to Digital Citizenship

SRSS believes that digital citizenship should reflect the same Human Values cultivated through everyday education.

:::values
- **Truth:** Using digital technologies honestly, accurately, and responsibly.
- **Right Conduct:** Respecting rules, boundaries, privacy, and the rights of others.
- **Peace:** Communicating calmly and resolving disagreements constructively.
- **Love:** Demonstrating kindness, empathy, and consideration in digital interactions.
- **Nonviolence:** Rejecting bullying, intimidation, exploitation, and harmful behaviour.
:::

Through these values, the School seeks to help pupils develop responsible habits that extend beyond the classroom and into their wider digital lives.
