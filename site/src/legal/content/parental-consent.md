## 1. Introduction

The SRSS Digital Hub (“the Site”, “the Platform”, “the Hub”, “we”, “our”, or “us”, as applicable) is an evolving digital platform established under the auspices of the Sathya Sai International Organisation of Malaysia (SSIOM) to support the educational, administrative, and community activities of Sekolah Rendah Sathya Sai (SRSS).

The Platform is developed as part of the School’s progressive digital transformation, commencing with SRSS25, its inaugural Silver Jubilee initiative, and is intended to provide a responsible, secure, and accessible digital environment for pupils, parents, guardians, teachers, administrators, and authorised members of the school community.

As a private primary school serving children, SRSS recognises that digital participation requires particular care in relation to parental authority, children’s privacy, personal data protection, educational responsibilities, online safeguarding, and informed consent.

This Parental Consent, Guardian Authority & Children’s Digital Participation Policy (“Policy”) establishes the principles governing parental and guardian authorisation, pupil access to digital services, the collection and management of consent, the exercise of parental responsibilities, and the protection of children’s rights and welfare within the Platform.

The Policy also explains how the School may verify parental or guardianship authority, administer digital permissions, manage consent withdrawal, and respond to circumstances involving shared custody, guardianship restrictions, or other sensitive family arrangements.

The Policy applies to parents, legal guardians, pupils, teachers, school administrators, authorised service providers, and other persons participating in or administering the Platform.

It shall be read together with the SRSS Digital Hub [Terms of Use](/legal/terms-of-use), [Privacy Policy](/legal/privacy-policy), [Child Protection & Online Safeguarding Policy](/legal/online-safeguarding), [Photography, Video, Student Work & Media Consent Policy](/legal/media-consent), [Data Protection, Information Security & Records Retention Policy](/legal/data-protection-retention), and other applicable institutional policies.

The School recognises that parental consent is an important safeguard but does not, by itself, remove the School’s independent responsibilities concerning the safety, dignity, education, privacy, and welfare of children.

Guided by the philosophy of Sathya Sai Educare, the Platform seeks to cultivate responsible digital participation grounded in the five universal Human Values of Truth, Right Conduct, Peace, Love, and Nonviolence.

## 2. Purpose and Scope of This Policy

This Policy seeks to establish clear and responsible arrangements for children’s participation in the Platform. Its objectives include:

- Protecting the privacy, dignity, and welfare of pupils.
- Recognising the legitimate rights and responsibilities of parents and legal guardians.
- Establishing appropriate parental authorisation procedures.
- Preventing unauthorised access to children’s personal and educational information.
- Supporting safe, age-appropriate digital participation.
- Providing clear distinctions between essential educational processing and optional digital activities.
- Establishing procedures for granting, reviewing, and withdrawing consent.
- Addressing guardianship, custody, and family-related access restrictions.
- Supporting accountability in the management of pupil accounts and digital permissions.
- Promoting responsible digital citizenship consistent with the School’s Human Values philosophy.

This Policy applies to current pupils and, where relevant, prospective pupils, former pupils, and other children whose information may be processed through authorised Platform activities.

## 3. Recognition of Children as Protected Users

SRSS recognises that primary-school pupils require enhanced care when participating in digital environments. Children may not fully understand the implications of online information sharing, public publication, digital permanence, account security, or interactions with third-party services.

Accordingly, the School shall endeavour to ensure that pupil-facing functions are designed and administered in a manner appropriate to children’s ages, developmental needs, and educational circumstances.

The School should avoid imposing unnecessary contractual or administrative responsibilities directly upon children. Where legally required, appropriate parental or guardian authorisation shall be obtained.

However, parental authorisation shall not be treated as permission to expose children to inappropriate, unsafe, exploitative, or otherwise unlawful digital practices. The School’s safeguarding obligations remain applicable regardless of whether parental consent has been provided.

## 4. Definition of Parent and Legal Guardian

For the purposes of this Policy, a “Parent” or “Legal Guardian” refers to an individual who possesses the relevant legal authority to make decisions concerning a child under applicable Malaysian law. Such authority may arise through parenthood, lawful guardianship, a court order, or another legally recognised arrangement.

A person who provides financial support, resides with a child, or assists with school matters does not necessarily possess unrestricted legal authority over the child’s educational records or personal information.

The School may distinguish between:

- A parent with relevant legal authority.
- A legally appointed guardian.
- A person authorised to receive school communications.
- An emergency contact.
- A person authorised to collect the pupil.
- Another individual granted limited administrative access.

These categories do not automatically confer identical rights. The School shall determine access and authorisation according to applicable law, verified documentation, and legitimate educational or safeguarding requirements.

## 5. Verification of Parental or Guardianship Authority

Before granting access to restricted pupil information or accepting significant consent instructions, the School may undertake reasonable verification of the individual’s authority. Verification may involve:

- Existing school enrolment records.
- Parent or guardian identification.
- Verified contact information.
- Relevant guardianship documentation.
- Court orders or other legal documents, where applicable.
- Confirmation through authorised school administrative procedures.
- Additional verification where conflicting information exists.

Verification should be proportionate to the nature and sensitivity of the requested access or decision. The School should not collect unnecessary identification documents where existing verified records are sufficient.

Documents containing sensitive family information must be handled confidentially and made accessible only to authorised personnel.

## 6. Shared Parental Responsibility and Custody Arrangements

The School recognises that pupils may live within different family and guardianship arrangements. These may include shared parental responsibility, separation, divorce, sole custody, legal guardianship, or other circumstances.

The School shall not automatically assume that one parent has exclusive authority merely because that parent completed the enrolment process or is the primary school contact. Where relevant legal arrangements exist, the School may request appropriate documentation to determine the scope of parental or guardianship authority.

### 6.1 Shared Parental Authority

Where more than one person possesses relevant legal authority, the School shall determine the appropriate arrangements for access, consent, and communications according to applicable law and verified records.

### 6.2 Restricted Access

Where a valid legal restriction applies, the School may limit access to pupil information or digital services accordingly.

### 6.3 Conflicting Instructions

Where authorised adults provide conflicting instructions, the School may temporarily restrict disputed optional actions while seeking clarification or appropriate documentation. Such restrictions should not unnecessarily interfere with the child’s essential education or welfare.

### 6.4 Safeguarding Considerations

Where disclosure of a child’s information could create a credible safeguarding risk, the School shall take appropriate protective measures consistent with applicable law.

## 7. Parental Registration and Digital Accounts

The Platform may progressively provide authenticated accounts for parents and legal guardians. Such accounts may allow access to approved school services, including:

- School announcements.
- Academic information.
- Attendance information.
- Administrative forms.
- Parent communications.
- Consent management.
- Educational updates.
- Other authorised services.

Account registration may require identity verification and confirmation of the user’s relationship to the relevant pupil.

The School may restrict or revoke access where an account is used without authorisation, where legal authority changes, or where access presents a legitimate security or safeguarding concern.

Parents and guardians must not share their credentials with unauthorised persons.

## 8. Pupil Digital Accounts

The School may provide age-appropriate digital accounts to pupils for legitimate educational purposes. These accounts may support learning activities, assignment submission, access to approved educational resources, and other authorised functions.

Pupil accounts should be configured according to the child’s age, educational needs, and applicable safeguarding requirements.

### 8.1 Account Creation

Pupil accounts should ordinarily be created or authorised through approved school procedures. Where legally required, appropriate parental or guardian authorisation shall be obtained.

### 8.2 Account Permissions

Pupil permissions should be limited to functions necessary for legitimate educational participation.

### 8.3 Account Supervision

The School may provide appropriate teacher or administrative supervision of pupil-facing functions.

### 8.4 Account Termination

When a pupil leaves the School, access privileges may be suspended or terminated according to approved procedures. Relevant educational records may continue to be retained where justified by applicable legal or institutional requirements.

## 9. Categories of Parental Consent and Authorisation

The School recognises that different digital activities may require different forms of consent, authorisation, notice, or other lawful justification. Consent for one purpose does not automatically authorise unrelated processing.

The Platform may progressively support the following categories.

### 9.1 Essential School Administration

Information may be processed where necessary and lawful for enrolment, educational administration, school communications, attendance, and other legitimate school responsibilities. The School shall identify the appropriate legal basis for such processing.

Where consent is not the applicable legal basis, the School should not misleadingly describe the processing as optional.

### 9.2 Educational Digital Services

Certain digital tools may be used to support teaching, learning, and assessment. Where parental consent is legally required, the School shall obtain appropriate authorisation.

### 9.3 Photography and Media Publication

Separate permissions may be required for public publication of pupil photographs, videos, performances, and creative work. These arrangements are further governed by the [Photography, Video, Student Work & Media Consent Policy](/legal/media-consent).

### 9.4 Optional Activities and Programmes

Additional authorisation may be required for optional programmes, external collaborations, or other activities beyond ordinary school administration.

### 9.5 Third-Party Digital Services

Where a third-party service involves additional processing of pupil information, the School shall assess applicable legal, contractual, privacy, and safeguarding requirements.

### 9.6 Sensitive Information

Processing of sensitive personal information, including certain health or welfare information, requires particular care and an appropriate legal basis.

## 10. Informed Consent

Where consent is relied upon, the School shall endeavour to ensure that it is informed, sufficiently specific, and appropriately documented. Parents and guardians should receive understandable information concerning:

- The purpose of the proposed activity.
- The categories of information involved.
- The intended recipients.
- Whether information may be publicly accessible.
- Whether external service providers are involved.
- Relevant risks and safeguards.
- Applicable retention arrangements.
- Available withdrawal procedures.
- Any consequences of declining optional participation.

Consent should not be obtained through misleading descriptions or unnecessary pressure.

The School should distinguish clearly between activities that are genuinely optional and processing that is necessary and lawful for the provision of essential educational services.

## 11. Electronic Consent and Digital Authorisation

The SRSS Digital Hub may progressively incorporate electronic consent functions. Where implemented, these may allow verified parents or guardians to review and manage selected permissions through authenticated accounts.

Electronic consent records may include:

- Pupil identity.
- Verified parent or guardian identity.
- Consent category.
- Description of the authorised activity.
- Version of the relevant consent wording.
- Date and time of authorisation.
- Consent status.
- Subsequent amendments.
- Withdrawal history.
- Relevant administrative verification information.

Electronic authorisation mechanisms shall be assessed for legal suitability, reliability, and security. Where a particular activity requires additional documentation or a more formal authorisation process, the School may require such measures.

## 12. Consent Records and Auditability

The School may retain appropriate records of consent and authorisation to support accountability and demonstrate compliance. Such records should identify the scope of permission granted.

The School should avoid treating an outdated or ambiguous consent record as unrestricted authorisation for newly introduced purposes. Where a consent form is materially revised, the School shall assess whether renewed authorisation is required.

Consent records shall be protected against unauthorised alteration and disclosure. Access should be restricted to authorised personnel.

## 13. Withdrawal of Consent

Where processing depends on consent, a parent or guardian with the appropriate authority may request withdrawal in accordance with applicable law. Withdrawal requests should identify the relevant pupil and processing activity where reasonably possible.

Following receipt of a valid request, the School shall assess the applicable legal requirements and take appropriate action. Such action may include:

- Updating consent records.
- Discontinuing future optional processing.
- Restricting further publication.
- Removing content from School-controlled channels where required.
- Notifying relevant authorised personnel.
- Reviewing third-party processing arrangements.

Withdrawal does not necessarily invalidate processing lawfully undertaken before withdrawal. The School may also be required or permitted to retain certain information for legitimate legal, educational, safeguarding, or administrative purposes.

Where consent is not the applicable legal basis for essential processing, the School shall explain the distinction.

## 14. Refusal of Optional Consent

Parents and guardians may decline optional activities or publication permissions where participation is genuinely voluntary.

A refusal to authorise optional public media publication should not ordinarily prevent a pupil from participating in essential educational activities. The School may make reasonable arrangements to respect such choices.

Where a particular optional activity cannot reasonably be provided without specified processing, the School should explain the consequences of declining participation.

The School shall not represent optional consent as compulsory where no applicable legal or operational justification exists.

## 15. Respect for the Child’s Views

SRSS recognises that children should be treated with dignity and respect. Where appropriate to a pupil’s age, maturity, and circumstances, the School should explain digital activities in language the child can understand.

The School should consider the child’s views concerning optional publication, recordings, online participation, and other activities affecting personal dignity.

Parental consent does not justify ignoring a child’s significant distress or credible safeguarding concerns. Where a child objects to optional public publication of personal media, the School should review the proposed use.

The child’s views shall be considered alongside applicable law, parental authority, and the School’s safeguarding responsibilities.

## 16. Age-Appropriate Digital Participation

Digital services should be designed and administered with consideration for the developmental needs of primary-school pupils. The School may implement measures including:

- Simplified interfaces.
- Limited account permissions.
- Teacher-assisted access.
- Restricted communication functions.
- Appropriate content filtering.
- Clear safety instructions.
- Supervised educational activities.
- Reduced collection of unnecessary personal information.

The School should avoid exposing younger pupils to digital functions that are not reasonably necessary for their educational participation.

## 17. Online Communications Involving Pupils

The Platform may progressively support educational communications involving pupils and teachers. Such communications should take place through approved channels and be subject to appropriate supervision.

Staff should not use unauthorised private messaging channels to conduct school-related communications with pupils where such use conflicts with safeguarding procedures.

The School may establish rules concerning:

- Permitted communication hours.
- Approved communication channels.
- Group communications.
- Recordkeeping.
- Parent involvement.
- Reporting inappropriate contact.
- Restrictions on sharing personal contact details.

Communications should remain professional, respectful, and consistent with the School’s safeguarding responsibilities.

## 18. Pupil Privacy and Confidentiality

Pupil information must be treated as confidential except where disclosure is authorised, lawful, and appropriate.

Parents and guardians should ordinarily receive access only to information concerning children for whom they possess the relevant authority. Access to one child’s records does not confer access to information concerning classmates or other families.

The School should avoid displaying unnecessary identifying information in shared digital environments. Sensitive welfare, medical, disciplinary, and safeguarding information requires additional access restrictions.

## 19. Academic and Educational Information

The Platform may provide parents and guardians with access to approved academic information. Such information may include:

- Attendance records.
- Assessment results.
- Academic reports.
- Teacher feedback.
- Learning progress.
- Co-curricular participation.
- Educational achievements.

Access shall be determined according to verified authority, applicable law, and school procedures.

Parents should not use information obtained through the Platform to harass, intimidate, or publicly shame pupils, teachers, or other individuals.

Where records contain information about more than one pupil, the School should take reasonable steps to protect the confidentiality of others.

## 20. Behavioural, Welfare and Human Values Records

The School may maintain educational observations relating to pupil behaviour, welfare, and personal development. Human Values education may include reflection upon Truth, Right Conduct, Peace, Love, and Nonviolence.

Such observations should be used constructively to support education and personal growth. They should not be presented as definitive judgments of a child’s moral worth or future character.

The School should avoid public ranking, humiliation, or stigmatisation based on behavioural or Human Values observations. Access to sensitive records should be restricted to authorised individuals.

## 21. Medical and Emergency Information

The School may collect relevant medical, allergy, emergency contact, or health-related information where necessary for legitimate school purposes. Such information may be essential to the child’s safety and welfare.

The School shall identify the appropriate legal basis for processing sensitive information and apply relevant safeguards. Access should be restricted to personnel with a legitimate need to know.

Parents and guardians are encouraged to notify the School of material changes affecting a child’s health or emergency arrangements.

The Platform is not intended to replace emergency medical services.

## 22. Photography, Video and Student Work

Pupil photographs, recordings, performances, artwork, and other creative contributions may be used for authorised educational or institutional purposes.

The School shall distinguish between internal educational use, restricted community sharing, and public publication. Public promotional use may require separate permission. The School shall also consider copyright ownership, privacy, safeguarding, and the child’s wishes.

The full provisions governing such activities are set out in the [Photography, Video, Student Work & Media Consent Policy](/legal/media-consent).

## 23. Third-Party Educational Platforms

The School may use approved external educational technologies, including Google Workspace for Education and other authorised services. Before introducing a new service involving children’s information, the School should assess:

- Its educational purpose.
- The information required.
- The provider’s privacy and security arrangements.
- Relevant contractual terms.
- Age-appropriate account settings.
- Applicable parental authorisation requirements.
- Data retention and deletion arrangements.
- Cross-border processing considerations.
- Safeguarding implications.
- Available alternatives where appropriate.

The School should not assume that parental consent alone resolves every legal or safeguarding issue associated with third-party processing.

## 24. Prohibition of Unauthorised Commercial Profiling

The Platform is intended primarily for educational and institutional purposes. Children’s personal information must not be used for unrelated commercial profiling, targeted behavioural advertising, or unauthorised data brokerage through School-controlled services.

Any proposed processing beyond legitimate educational or administrative purposes must undergo appropriate institutional and legal review. The School shall endeavour to minimise unnecessary tracking of pupils.

## 25. Artificial Intelligence and Automated Processing

The School may consider introducing artificial intelligence or automated technologies as part of future Platform development. Any such introduction should be subject to appropriate educational, privacy, security, and safeguarding assessment.

The School should not upload confidential pupil records to unauthorised artificial intelligence services.

Where automated technologies are used to support educational decisions, appropriate human oversight should be maintained. Automated outputs should not be treated as definitive judgments concerning a child’s academic ability, welfare, behaviour, or character.

Where required by law, appropriate notice or authorisation shall be obtained.

This provision does not imply that artificial intelligence functions are currently active within the Platform.

## 26. Parental Responsibilities for Digital Safety

Parents and guardians play an important role in supporting children’s safe digital participation. They are encouraged to:

- Supervise age-appropriate Platform use.
- Protect account credentials.
- Discuss responsible online behaviour.
- Encourage respectful communication.
- Report inappropriate content or contact.
- Avoid sharing confidential school information.
- Respect the privacy of other pupils.
- Keep relevant contact and guardianship information current.
- Follow authorised school communication procedures.

Parents should avoid allowing unauthorised persons to use their accounts to access pupil information.

The School may provide guidance or awareness materials to support responsible digital participation.

## 27. Responsible Digital Citizenship

SRSS encourages pupils to practise responsible digital citizenship in accordance with the five universal Human Values.

:::values
- **Truth:** Communicating honestly and avoiding deliberate misinformation or impersonation.
- **Right Conduct:** Respecting school rules, privacy, intellectual property, and authorised digital boundaries.
- **Peace:** Using technology in ways that support thoughtful, respectful, and constructive interaction.
- **Love:** Demonstrating kindness, empathy, and consideration toward others.
- **Nonviolence:** Rejecting cyberbullying, harassment, intimidation, exploitation, and harmful digital behaviour.
:::

Digital citizenship education should be age-appropriate and focused on learning, reflection, and positive development.

## 28. Cyberbullying and Inappropriate Digital Conduct

The School does not condone cyberbullying, harassment, intimidation, discriminatory abuse, or the unauthorised sharing of harmful content. Concerns may involve:

- Threatening messages.
- Repeated harassment.
- Humiliating photographs or recordings.
- Impersonation.
- Unauthorised disclosure of personal information.
- Deliberate exclusion or intimidation through digital services.
- Other harmful online behaviour.

Such concerns should be reported through appropriate school channels. Where serious safeguarding risks arise, the School shall consider prompt protective measures and applicable reporting obligations.

Responses involving pupils should consider age, maturity, circumstances, educational needs, and child welfare.

## 29. Parent Access and Changes in Family Circumstances

Parents and guardians should notify the School of material changes affecting legal authority, authorised contacts, or access to pupil information. Such changes may include:

- New guardianship arrangements.
- Court orders.
- Changes in authorised contact persons.
- Changes in custody arrangements.
- Restrictions on disclosure.
- Other relevant legal circumstances.

The School may request appropriate documentation before changing access permissions. Access changes should be implemented in a manner that protects the child’s welfare and complies with applicable law.

## 30. Emergency and Safeguarding Exceptions

There may be circumstances in which the School is legally required or otherwise lawfully authorised to process or disclose information without ordinary parental consent. Such circumstances may include serious safeguarding concerns, legal obligations, or situations requiring appropriate protective action.

Any such processing or disclosure must be limited to what is lawful, necessary, and proportionate in the circumstances.

The School shall not interpret parental consent requirements as preventing mandatory reporting or appropriate protective action. Where immediate danger exists, appropriate emergency services or authorities should be contacted.

## 31. Complaints and Disputes Concerning Parental Authority

Disputes concerning account access, consent, guardianship, or disclosure of pupil information may be referred to the Principal or appropriately authorised school personnel. The School may request relevant documentation to assess the matter.

Where a dispute cannot be resolved through ordinary administrative procedures, it may be referred to an authorised representative of Sekolah Rendah Sathya Berhad.

The School shall not purport to determine contested legal custody rights beyond its authority. Where necessary, the parties may be directed to obtain appropriate legal clarification.

Safeguarding concerns shall be handled according to applicable child protection procedures.

## 32. Records of Parental Instructions

The School may maintain records of significant parental or guardian instructions concerning digital participation. Such records may include:

- Account access approvals.
- Consent decisions.
- Withdrawal requests.
- Guardianship verification.
- Relevant access restrictions.
- Communication preferences.
- Administrative decisions.
- Significant disputes or clarifications.

These records shall be protected according to applicable privacy and information security requirements. Access should be limited to authorised personnel.

Records should be retained only for as long as justified by applicable legal, educational, safeguarding, or administrative requirements.

## 33. Transition When a Pupil Leaves the School

When a pupil withdraws, transfers, or completes primary education at SRSS, the School may adjust or terminate access to pupil and parent services. The School should consider appropriate arrangements for:

- Final academic records.
- Administrative clearances.
- Relevant school communications.
- Account closure.
- Retention of official records.
- Withdrawal of optional permissions.
- Secure handling of historical information.

The termination of Platform access does not automatically require immediate deletion of all pupil records. Retention and disposal shall be governed by applicable law and the [Data Protection, Information Security & Records Retention Policy](/legal/data-protection-retention).

## 34. Transition to Adulthood and Former Pupil Rights

Certain records may continue to be retained after a pupil leaves SRSS. As former pupils mature and attain adulthood, their rights concerning personal information may differ from those exercised on their behalf during childhood.

The School shall assess requests from former pupils according to applicable law, identity verification requirements, and the nature of the information concerned.

Historical parental authorisation should not automatically be treated as unrestricted permission for new uses of an adult former pupil’s personal information. This consideration is particularly relevant to alumni engagement, historical publications, and the SRSS25 Silver Jubilee initiative.

## 35. Changes to Digital Services

The SRSS Digital Hub is intended to evolve through phased development. New functions may include expanded pupil services, parental communications, digital forms, learning portfolios, consent management, and other authorised school management features.

Before introducing functions involving new categories of personal information or materially different processing purposes, the School should assess the applicable privacy, consent, security, and safeguarding requirements. Where necessary, additional notices or permissions shall be obtained.

The introduction of a new Platform function does not automatically extend earlier consent to unrelated purposes.

## 36. Amendments to This Policy

This Policy may be revised to reflect changes in:

- Applicable Malaysian legislation.
- Education-related requirements.
- Child protection obligations.
- Parental consent procedures.
- Guardianship verification practices.
- Platform functionality.
- Third-party services.
- Institutional governance.
- Emerging digital safeguarding risks.

Material changes shall be communicated through appropriate channels. Where changes introduce new processing activities requiring consent, the School shall undertake the necessary procedures.

The latest effective date and revision date will be displayed on this page.

## 37. Governing Law

This Policy shall be governed by and interpreted in accordance with the laws of Malaysia. Relevant legislation may include, where applicable:

- Education Act 1996 (Act 550).
- Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Act 709), as amended.
- Child Act 2001 (Act 611).
- Guardianship of Infants Act 1961 (Act 351), where applicable.
- Law Reform (Marriage and Divorce) Act 1976 (Act 164), where applicable.
- Electronic Commerce Act 2006 (Act 658), where applicable.
- Other relevant Malaysian legislation and legal requirements.

The applicability of particular guardianship and family-law provisions may depend on the child’s and family’s circumstances, including the relevant personal-law framework.

Nothing in this Policy shall override a valid court order, applicable statutory requirement, or right that cannot lawfully be excluded.
