## 1. Introduction

Sekolah Rendah Sathya Sai (SRSS) is committed to providing a safe, nurturing and respectful environment in which every child is valued and protected. This Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy (“Policy”) sets out how the School protects pupils from abuse and neglect, in school, at school activities and online, and how staff, volunteers and governors must respond to any concern.

The Board of Governors of Sekolah Rendah Sathya Berhad and the Council of the Sathya Sai International Organisation of Malaysia (SSIOM) take seriously their responsibility to safeguard and promote the welfare of children, and to work together with other agencies to ensure adequate arrangements within SRSS to identify, assess and support those children who are suffering, or are likely to suffer, harm, and to provide support to those with additional needs.

This Policy, and the School’s other safeguarding policies, have been developed in accordance with the principles established by:

- The Child Act 2001 (Act 611).
- The Criminal Procedure Code (Act 593).
- The National Child Protection Policy (2009).
- The Adoption Act 1952 (Act 257).
- The Child Care Centres Act 1984 (Act 308).
- The Children and Young Persons (Employment) Act 1966 (Act 350).
- The Domestic Violence Act 1994 (Act 521).
- The Education Act 1996 (Act 550).
- The Islamic Family Law (Federal Territories) Act 1984 (Act 303), incorporating its latest amendments.

The Policy applies to all staff, volunteers, governors, contractors and visitors who work with or come into contact with SRSS pupils. Online safety is addressed in more detail in the [Child Protection & Online Safeguarding Policy](/legal/online-safeguarding), which applies this Policy to the SRSS Digital Hub and other digital services.

## 2. Aims of This Policy

The aims of this Policy are:

- To support each child’s development in ways that will foster security, confidence and independence.
- To promote the pupil’s voice, and ensure that all staff listen to children.
- To provide an environment in which children feel safe, secure, valued and respected, and feel confident — and know how — to approach adults if they are in difficulties, believing they will be listened to effectively.
- To raise the awareness of all teaching and non-teaching staff of the need to safeguard children and of their responsibilities in recognising and providing early help, and identifying and reporting possible cases of abuse.
- To provide a systematic means of monitoring and supporting children known or thought to be at risk of harm, and ensure that the School contributes to assessments of need and support packages for those children.
- To emphasise the need for good levels of communication between all members of staff.
- To maintain a structured procedure within the School that will be followed by all members of the SRSS community in cases of suspected abuse.
- To develop and promote effective working relationships with other agencies, especially the police and the Department of Social Welfare (Jabatan Kebajikan Masyarakat, JKM).
- To ensure that all adults working in the SRSS community who have substantial access to children have been checked as to their suitability, and given basic induction regarding child protection and what constitutes safe practice with children.

## 3. Governance and Guiding Principles

The Board of Governors of Sekolah Rendah Sathya Berhad uses the following as its guiding principles:

- All members of the Board of Governors understand and fulfil their responsibilities.
- The School has a designated senior member of staff who monitors the School’s child protection procedures.
- The School has a designated senior member of staff — the Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL) — who has undertaken the NSPCC Designated Safeguarding Lead training or equivalent, and who undertakes other training as necessary, including refresher training at two-yearly intervals.
- The School has at least one other senior member of staff who will act in the DSL’s absence, and who has also undertaken safeguarding children training, updated at two-yearly intervals.
- All members of staff are familiar with the procedures to be followed when a suspicion or allegation of abuse or neglect of a child is first encountered.
- The Principal and all members of staff undertake training in child protection, approved and delivered by the DSL (or a deputy), to develop their understanding of the signs and indicators of abuse and to equip them with the knowledge and skills necessary to fulfil their responsibilities for safeguarding children.
- Such knowledge and skills are kept up to date by an annual refresher briefing on the School’s procedures, and by more thorough training at least every three years.
- All new members of staff, regular volunteers and governors are given a copy of this Policy, which contains the School’s arrangements and the names of the key safeguarding leads, together with the School’s guidance for staff, as part of their induction.
- Temporary members of staff who may have contact with pupils are also given safeguarding training, the names of the key safeguarding leads, and guidance on appropriate conduct.
- All members of staff, volunteers and governors know how to respond to a pupil who discloses abuse, or to information received from any source indicating that a child may be at risk of harm.
- All parents and guardians are made aware of the responsibilities of staff members with regard to child protection through the publication of this Policy, which is available on the SRSS website and on request to parents and guardians of current and prospective pupils.
- Community users and others who organise activities for children on School premises are aware of the School’s safeguarding guidelines and procedures.
- The selection and recruitment of staff include checks of their suitability to work with children with the relevant Malaysian authorities. Any member of staff found unsuitable to work with children will be reported to the relevant authorities and professional bodies.
- This Policy and its procedures are reviewed and updated annually.
- Any deficiencies or weaknesses in the School’s child protection arrangements will be remedied without delay.
- The Board of Governors, taking into account an annual report on all child protection matters prepared by the DSL, undertakes an annual review of the School’s safeguarding policies and procedures to ensure that all responsibilities related to this aspect of the School’s governance have been discharged.

## 5. Responsibilities of the Designated Safeguarding Lead and Deputies

The DSL and the Deputy DSLs are responsible for:

- Acting as a source of support, advice and expertise for staff and others to discuss concerns about possible abuse of children.
- Having undertaken the NSPCC Designated Safeguarding Lead training or equivalent, and other training as necessary, including refresher training at two-yearly intervals. The Deputy DSLs are senior members of staff who act in the DSL’s absence and have also undertaken safeguarding children training, updated at two-yearly intervals.
- Promoting and supporting the provision of early help and intervention where concerns have not reached a child protection threshold, using early help assessments where appropriate.
- Co-ordinating referrals of a child for whom there are concerns to the relevant outside agencies.
- Ensuring that a referral is made as soon as possible, and within 24 hours of any allegation being made.
- Emailing a written record of the referral, and following up for a response or further action where necessary.
- Keeping written records of concerns about a child even where there is no need to make an immediate referral.
- Liaising with other agencies and professionals.
- Encouraging a culture of listening to children and taking account of their wishes and feelings, among all staff, in any measures the School may put in place to protect them.
- Reviewing and updating this Policy in line with changes in legislation and recommended best practice.
- Keeping the member of staff who raised a concern informed of relevant developments, especially enquiries by JKM and police investigations.
- Providing, in consultation with the Principal, an annual report for the Board of Governors and the SSIOM Council detailing any changes to the policy and procedures, the training undertaken by the DSL and by all staff and governors, the number and type of incidents and cases, and the number (but not the names) of children with child protection plans.

## 6. Recognising Abuse and Neglect

All members of staff should be alert to the possibility of signs of abuse or neglect of any pupil — by parents, siblings, other relatives, guardians, other carers, friends, acquaintances, strangers and members of the SRSS community, including staff and fellow pupils.

Abuse or neglect of a child can arise through inflicting harm or by failing to act to prevent harm, and can take many forms. The following categories are used.

### 6.1 Emotional Abuse

Emotional abuse is the persistent ill-treatment or rejection of a child such as to cause severe and adverse effects on the child’s emotional development. It may involve conveying to children that they are worthless or unloved, inadequate, or valued only insofar as they meet the needs of another person. It may include not giving the child opportunities to express their views, deliberately silencing them, or “making fun” of what they say or how they communicate.

It may feature age or developmentally inappropriate expectations being imposed on children. These may include interactions that are beyond the child’s developmental capacity, as well as overprotection and limitation of exploration and learning, or preventing the child participating in normal social interaction. It may involve seeing or hearing the ill-treatment of another. It may involve serious bullying (including cyberbullying), causing children frequently to feel frightened or in danger, or the exploitation or corruption of children.

Some level of emotional abuse is involved in all types of maltreatment of a child, though it may occur alone.

### 6.2 Physical Abuse

Physical abuse may involve hitting, shaking, throwing, poisoning, burning or scalding, drowning, suffocating, or otherwise causing physical harm to a child. Physical harm may also be caused when a parent or carer fabricates the symptoms of, or deliberately induces, illness in a child.

### 6.3 Sexual Abuse

Sexual abuse involves forcing or enticing a child to take part in sexual activities, not necessarily involving a high level of violence, whether or not the child is aware of what is happening. The activities may involve physical contact, including assault by penetration or non-penetrative acts such as kissing, rubbing and touching outside of clothing.

They may also include non-contact activities, such as involving children in looking at, or in the production of, sexual images, watching sexual activities, encouraging children to behave in sexually inappropriate ways, or grooming a child in preparation for abuse (including via the internet).

Sexual abuse is not solely perpetrated by adult males. Women can also commit acts of sexual abuse, as can other children.

### 6.4 Neglect

Neglect is the persistent failure to meet a child’s basic physical and/or psychological needs, likely to result in the serious impairment of the child’s health or development. Neglect may involve a parent or carer failing to:

- Provide adequate food, clothing and shelter (including exclusion from home or abandonment).
- Protect a child from physical and emotional harm or danger.
- Ensure adequate supervision (including the use of inadequate care-givers).
- Ensure access to appropriate medical care or treatment.

It may also include neglect of, or unresponsiveness to, a child’s basic emotional needs.

### 6.5 Possible Signs of Abuse

Abuse can occur in a variety of circumstances and across all socio-economic groups. Children may be particularly vulnerable where there is a history of family violence, bullying, drug or alcohol abuse, mental health problems, learning difficulties or unemployment. Identification of abuse is rarely straightforward, because indications often comprise a complex mixture of medical symptoms, behavioural characteristics and background factors.

The following characteristics in a pupil can be indications of potential abuse:

- Unexplained or unusual injuries (for example, in inaccessible sites like the neck, behind the ears, or on the soles of the feet).
- Cuts, bite marks, scalds or bruising.
- The apparent age of injuries not being consistent with the account given.
- Evidence of injuries being repeated.
- Evidence of poor overall care and a failure to thrive (for example, the pupil appears dirty and unkempt).
- Swallowing harmful substances or consuming inappropriate food or drink.
- Evidence of self-harm.
- Unexplained deterioration in performance at school.
- Evidence of sexual knowledge or activity that is inappropriate for the pupil’s age.
- Behavioural or sudden changes in behaviour (for example, aggression, hyperactivity, nervousness or social withdrawal).

The presence of one or more of these characteristics can only indicate — not confirm with any certainty — that abuse may have taken place. Behavioural problems, for instance, are often associated with other conditions.

### 6.6 Conduct of Parents and Carers

The conduct of parents can also be indicative. Inconsistent explanations of their child’s injuries, a lack of concern for or general detachment from their child’s problems, a refusal or reluctance to allow treatment or discuss problems, and displays of aggressive behaviour towards their child should all alert staff to the possibility of abuse or neglect.

### 6.7 Abuse by Other Children

SRSS recognises that children can be vulnerable to abuse by their peers that falls outside the category of mistreatment that would be dealt with as bullying. Such abuse will be taken as seriously as abuse perpetrated by an adult.

Staff should be alert to the difference between appropriate and abusive (exploitative) peer relationships. They should not dismiss abusive sexual behaviour between children as “normal”, and should not develop high thresholds before reporting such behaviour to the DSL.

## 7. Action to Be Taken When Abuse Is Suspected

A member of staff — acting on personal observation or knowledge, or in response to an allegation — who has good reason to suspect that a pupil has been abused or neglected in any of the ways described above, or who has any other concern for a pupil’s wellbeing, has a duty to report the suspicion, allegation or concern to a senior member of staff without delay and without attempting to carry out any investigation.

The senior member of staff to whom the report is made would normally be the DSL. A member of staff may instinctively wish to discuss a concern first with the pupil’s class teacher. That would not be appropriate if any delay in referring the matter to an outside agency, including the police, might put the pupil’s welfare or safety at risk or jeopardise a full and proper investigation — for example, where there is bruising or another sign of injury that will quickly fade, so that examination by a properly qualified expert needs to be arranged urgently.

A suspicion of abuse or neglect often takes the form of a concern that cannot be based on hard factual evidence. Such a concern, when discussed with the DSL or other senior staff, may not necessarily prompt a referral, but may, along with concerns raised by others, help to create a bigger picture that suggests that the child is indeed at risk.

### 7.1 When a Pupil Wants to Tell You Something

A member of staff approached by a pupil who “wants to tell them something” should respond promptly and sympathetically, but must proceed with caution:

- The member of staff has a duty to listen to the pupil.
- Absolute confidentiality, whether requested by the pupil or not, must never be promised, because it cannot be guaranteed. If necessary, it should be made clear early in the conversation that any duty of confidence will not prevail over the duty to act on information suggesting that the welfare or safety of the pupil or any other person is at risk. If possible, the pupil’s agreement to further consultation should be obtained, but in any event the pupil should be reassured that information will only be passed on to those who need to know.
- If the pupil decides not to continue the conversation, they should be encouraged to talk to someone else, inside or outside the School.
- If the conversation involves any disclosure or allegation of a serious nature, notes should be made as fully and as quickly as possible, including the actual words used where appropriate, and the DSL must be consulted without delay.
- The role of the adult is to listen, to reassure and, if necessary, to make a written record of the conversation. They must not probe, ask leading questions, put words into the pupil’s mouth, or become involved in any form of medical examination. Misguided intervention can jeopardise a police investigation and impede later criminal proceedings.
- When the conversation has ended, any further comments or observations should be recorded straight away. Notes made at the time are of great value in any later inquiry or legal proceedings.

### 7.2 Records and Referral

The DSL will ensure that statements and any other relevant records that may be required as evidence are kept securely. These may be required by JKM or the police. Information relating to actual, alleged or suspected child abuse is subject to restricted disclosure.

Having received a report of actual, alleged or suspected abuse or neglect, or another major concern for a pupil’s welfare, the DSL may in certain circumstances proceed in a way that does not have the full support of the reporting member of staff — for example, where the DSL holds sensitive and confidential information about the pupil. In such cases the reporting member of staff should discuss their concerns with the Principal, and is in any event free to contact JKM or the police directly if they remain concerned and believe the DSL will not make a referral.

In all but inconsequential cases — such as an allegation that is demonstrably false or unfounded — the Principal will be informed.

## 8. Key Points for All Staff

All members of staff, including the Principal and senior leadership, must:

- Be alert to the possibility of abuse or neglect.
- Be able to recognise and act upon indications that a pupil’s welfare may be at risk.
- Know who to contact to discuss or express concerns about a child’s welfare.
- Be prepared to listen sympathetically to a pupil who wants to discuss a sensitive issue.
- Not promise absolute confidentiality.
- Not ask leading questions or put words into a pupil’s mouth.
- Not attempt to investigate suspicions of abuse or neglect or carry out any form of medical examination.
- Complete a written record of any disclosure by a child, or of any concern, and give it to the DSL by email or in hard copy.
- Report concerns about, or knowledge of, actual, alleged or suspected abuse or neglect to the DSL immediately. If the concern is about the DSL, it should be reported directly to the Principal.
- Report allegations involving a member of staff to the DSL or the Principal immediately.
- Only pass on information to others on a “need to know” basis.
- Feel free to make an external referral if necessary. Contact details for external agencies are in [section 23](#s-23).

## 9. Supporting Children

SRSS recognises that a child who is abused or witnesses violence may feel helpless and humiliated, may blame themselves, and may find it difficult to develop and maintain a sense of self-worth. The behaviour of a child in these circumstances may range from that which is perceived to be normal to aggressive or withdrawn. The School may provide the only stability in the lives of children who have been abused or who are at risk of harm.

SRSS will support all pupils by:

- Encouraging self-esteem and self-assertiveness — through the curriculum as well as through relationships between staff and pupils and between pupils themselves — while not condoning aggression or bullying.
- Using opportunities in Health Education and across the curriculum to make pupils aware of risks and to promote their ability to keep themselves safe, including age-appropriate personal safety and online safety education.
- Promoting a caring, safe and positive environment within the School.
- Offering a confidential counselling service that pupils can contact directly, with the School Counsellor working within this Policy when sharing information with the DSL.
- Liaising and working together with all other support services and agencies involved in the safeguarding of children.
- Notifying JKM as soon as there is a significant concern.
- Providing continuing support to a pupil about whom there have been concerns and who leaves the School, by ensuring that appropriate information is forwarded under confidential cover to the pupil’s new school, and that the School’s medical records are forwarded as a matter of priority.

## 10. Confidentiality and Information Sharing

SRSS recognises that all matters relating to child protection are confidential.

Anyone concerned with a suspected case of abuse should treat all personal information as confidential, taking particular care with sensitive information relating to the child and the child’s family. It may sometimes be necessary for one member of staff to disclose personal information about a pupil to another, but this will only be done on a “need to know” basis.

All staff must be aware that they have a professional responsibility to share information with colleagues and other agencies in order to safeguard children. Legal and professional obligations will not generally prevent the sharing of confidential information if:

- The pupil and/or the parent consent to the disclosure.
- The public interest in safeguarding the child’s welfare overrides the need to keep the information confidential.
- Disclosure is required by law or under an order of court.

All staff must be aware that they cannot promise a child to keep secrets that might compromise the child’s safety or wellbeing. In deciding whether there is a need to share information, the pupil’s best interests must be paramount.

The School will always share its intention to refer a child to JKM with the child’s parents or guardians, unless to do so could put the child at greater risk of harm or impede a criminal investigation. Where there is doubt, JKM should be consulted first.

## 11. Support for Staff

SRSS recognises that staff who have become involved with a child who has suffered harm, or appears likely to suffer harm, may find the situation stressful and upsetting. The School will support such staff by providing an opportunity to talk through their anxieties with the DSL and to seek further support as appropriate.

## 12. Allegations Against Staff

All staff must follow the School’s guidance for staff at all times. They should take care not to place themselves in a vulnerable position in their work with pupils, in both formal and informal settings.

It is always advisable for interviews or work with individual children or parents to be conducted in view of other adults where possible, or to notify a senior member of staff if a one-to-one session is planned. Communication with pupils and parents, by whatever means, including modern technology, should take place within professional boundaries. Pupils should never, for example, be accepted as “friends” on social networking sites. Where a pupil is already known to a member of staff through family or friendship, this should be made known to the member of staff’s line manager. Subjects of a personal nature should be avoided, and staff should be careful in their communications with pupils to avoid any possible misinterpretation of their motives or behaviour.

Volunteers who work with pupils (for example, parents accompanying an off-site visit) are expected to maintain standards of conduct comparable to those of employed staff.

A pupil may make an allegation against a member of staff or volunteer, or information may come to light raising a concern about a member of staff’s or volunteer’s suitability to work with children. Whoever receives such an allegation, whether directly from the pupil or through a third party, will immediately inform the Principal. This applies where an allegation indicates that a member of staff or other adult working with children has:

- Behaved in a way that has harmed a child, or may have harmed a child.
- Possibly committed a criminal offence against or related to a child.
- Behaved towards a child or children in a way that indicates they would pose a risk of harm to children.

Allegations of abuse made against staff, whether historical or current, are handled by the Principal and not by the DSL. If the allegation is against the Principal, the person receiving it must immediately inform the Chair of the Board of Governors of Sekolah Rendah Sathya Berhad, without notifying the Principal first.

When an allegation of abuse is made against a member of staff, it is referred directly to the relevant external agency (see [section 23](#s-23)) and their advice is followed. This initial conversation will establish the validity of the allegation. If a formal referral is needed, contact will be made with the relevant agency within 24 hours, and a strategy meeting will be called, which the Principal or the Chair of the Board of Governors should attend. A strategy meeting is any urgent formal discussion, which may take place by telephone, between senior leadership, the DSL, the police, JKM and education officers, requiring action before a first meeting.

The decision of the strategy meeting could be:

- A police investigation, if there is a criminal element to the allegation.
- A single-agency investigation carried out by the School.

The fact that a member of staff offers to resign does not prevent the allegation procedure reaching a conclusion.

After consultation with the relevant agencies, the parents or guardians of a child or children involved will be told about the allegation, provided the timing and content of that information have been agreed with the relevant external agency where JKM and the police are involved. They will be kept informed about the progress of the case, and told the outcome where there is no criminal prosecution. Under no circumstances will a child be sent home pending an investigation, unless exceptionally advised as a result of a strategy meeting.

The School will also keep the member of staff or other adult who is the subject of the allegation informed of the progress of the case, following any advice about the content and timing of information where the police are involved, and will consider what other support is appropriate for that person. The power to suspend a member of staff rests with the Principal and the Board of Governors. Suspension should not be automatic.

If the person who is the subject of an allegation resigns or ceases to provide their services, the allegation will still be followed up in accordance with this Policy. Where the School stops using a person’s services because they are considered unsuitable to work with children, the DSL will ensure that the relevant authorities are informed. The School will not enter into any settlement or compromise agreement that would prevent such a report from being made.

## 13. Whistleblowing

All staff should be aware of their duty to raise concerns about the management of child protection, which may include the attitude or actions of colleagues. They should speak in the first instance to the DSL or the Principal, to whom they must report any concern or allegation about school practices or the behaviour of colleagues that is likely to put pupils at risk of abuse or other serious harm. The School’s whistleblowing procedure is set out in a separate document.

SRSS recognises that children cannot be expected to raise concerns in an environment where staff fail to do so. Talian Kasih 15999 posters are displayed throughout the School. Talian Kasih is a helpline both for staff to raise concerns about pupils and for pupils to seek advice should they wish to.

## 14. Physical Intervention

The School’s policy on physical intervention by staff is set out separately. Staff may only ever use physical intervention as a last resort to prevent pupils from hurting themselves or others, damaging property or causing disorder, and must always use the minimum force necessary to prevent injury to another person.

Such events should be recorded, signed by a witness, and reported as soon as possible to the DSL so that the child’s parent can be notified the same day. Physical intervention that causes injury or distress to a child may be considered under child protection or disciplinary procedures.

## 15. Bullying

The School’s policy on bullying is set out in a separate document. Serious bullying, or allowing or condoning bullying, may lead to consideration under child protection procedures. This includes homophobic and gender-related bullying, and bullying related to any other personal characteristic.

SRSS recognises the rapid growth of cyberbullying: the use of electronic communications (such as email, mobile phone and text messaging, digital photography, instant messaging, personal websites, chat rooms, blogs and social networking sites) to harass, intimidate, insult, abuse, embarrass or otherwise harm or cause distress to others. As with all other forms of bullying, the School regards cyberbullying as totally unacceptable. See also the [Child Protection & Online Safeguarding Policy](/legal/online-safeguarding#s-8).

## 16. Complaints

SRSS has formal complaints procedures for both pupils and parents, described in the [Accessibility, Complaints & Digital Governance Policy](/legal/accessibility-complaints). Complaints and concerns relating to any safeguarding issue should be addressed in the first instance to the DSL, who will try to resolve the matter in a way that satisfies the complainant while balancing the rights and duties of pupils. Pupils will not be penalised for making a complaint in good faith.

## 17. Racist Incidents

SRSS acknowledges that repeated racist incidents, or a single serious incident, may lead to consideration under child protection procedures.

## 18. Missing Pupils

Where a pupil is unexpectedly absent from morning or afternoon registration, the DSL will make appropriate enquiries and, if thought necessary, arrange a search. If a pupil’s absence remains unexplained, staff should follow the School’s attendance procedures. The School recognises that irregular or missing attendance may indicate that a pupil is suffering abuse, and will be alert to that possibility.

## 19. Sexual Exploitation

Sexual exploitation involves exploitative situations, contexts and relationships in which young people receive something (for example food, accommodation, drugs, alcohol, gifts, money or, in some cases, simply affection) as a result of engaging in sexual activities. It ranges from seemingly consensual relationships to serious organised crime, but is always marked by an imbalance of power.

Exploitation may begin with what appears to be a caring relationship, which is attractive to children who are vulnerable to a loving approach. This risk exists for all children, and staff must be vigilant in identifying and reporting where it may be happening.

## 20. Online Safety

The same duties apply online. The [Child Protection & Online Safeguarding Policy](/legal/online-safeguarding) sets out the rules for communication between adults and pupils through digital channels, the publication of pupils’ images and information, cyberbullying, and the handling of concerns raised through the SRSS Digital Hub.

## 21. Safeguarding Records

Child protection and safeguarding records are kept separately from other pupil records, confidentially, and under restricted access. They are retained until the pupil reaches 25 years of age. Records of sexual abuse or other serious allegations are kept for the duration of any active inquiry, or for at least 30 years or until the individual concerned reaches normal retirement age, whichever is longer. Staff safeguarding training records are kept for 7 years after the staff member leaves the School.

The full retention schedule is in the [Data Protection, Information Security & Records Retention Policy](/legal/data-protection-retention#schedule-a-records-retention-periods).

## 22. Policy Review

This Policy and its procedures are reviewed and updated annually by the DSL and approved by the Board of Governors, taking into account the DSL’s annual report, changes in legislation and recommended best practice. Material changes will be communicated to staff, parents and guardians.
