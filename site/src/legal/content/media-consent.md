## 1. Introduction

Sekolah Rendah Sathya Sai (SRSS) recognises the importance of documenting and celebrating the educational journey, achievements, creativity, and personal development of its pupils. Photographs, videos, audio recordings, artwork, written compositions, performances, and other forms of media may serve valuable educational, historical, and community purposes.

The SRSS Digital Hub provides a digital environment through which selected school activities, achievements, educational materials, and institutional milestones may be documented and shared.

Its inaugural initiative, SRSS25, is introduced in conjunction with the School’s Silver Jubilee celebrations and may include historical photographs, anniversary publications, alumni contributions, commemorative events, and other media celebrating the School’s educational journey.

While recognising the value of such materials, SRSS places particular importance on the dignity, privacy, safety, and welfare of children.

This Photography, Video, Student Work & Media Consent Policy (“Policy”) establishes the principles governing the collection, recording, storage, use, publication, sharing, and withdrawal of media involving pupils and other members of the school community.

The Policy applies to school personnel, parents, guardians, pupils, volunteers, event organisers, photographers, media contributors, and other authorised persons involved in the creation or use of school-related media.

It shall be read together with the SRSS Digital Hub [Privacy Policy](/legal/privacy-policy), the [Parental Consent, Guardian Authority & Children’s Digital Participation Policy](/legal/parental-consent), the [Child Protection & Online Safeguarding Policy](/legal/online-safeguarding), and the [Intellectual Property, Educational Content & Acceptable Use Policy](/legal/intellectual-property).

## 2. Purpose of School Photography and Media

The School may create or use media for legitimate educational and institutional purposes. These may include:

- Documenting classroom activities.
- Recording educational projects.
- Celebrating pupil achievements.
- Supporting learning portfolios.
- Documenting co-curricular activities.
- Recording school events and performances.
- Preparing newsletters and publications.
- Supporting parent communications.
- Preserving institutional history.
- Celebrating the School’s Silver Jubilee.
- Promoting approved educational and community initiatives.
- Maintaining authorised historical archives.

Media collection should be proportionate to the intended purpose. The School should avoid unnecessary recording or publication of children, particularly where such activities may cause distress or create safeguarding risks.

## 3. Categories of Media Covered

This Policy applies to various forms of media, including:

### 3.1 Photographs

Individual portraits, group photographs, classroom photographs, event images, activity photographs, and other still images.

### 3.2 Video Recordings

Recordings of lessons, performances, presentations, school events, interviews, educational activities, and commemorative programmes.

### 3.3 Audio Recordings

Recordings of speeches, songs, performances, interviews, presentations, and other school-related audio content.

### 3.4 Student Artwork

Drawings, paintings, craftwork, digital artwork, and other visual creative works.

### 3.5 Written Work

Essays, poems, stories, reflections, project reports, and other original written materials.

### 3.6 Academic and Co-Curricular Achievements

Information or media relating to awards, competitions, projects, performances, service activities, and other recognised achievements.

### 3.7 Historical and Archival Materials

Photographs, recordings, publications, and other materials documenting the School’s history and development.

## 4. Categories of Media Use

SRSS distinguishes between different levels of media use. Consent or authorisation for one category does not automatically constitute permission for another.

### 4.1 Internal Educational Use

Media may be used within authorised school systems for legitimate educational activities. Examples include classroom documentation, teacher assessment, learning portfolios, and internal school records.

Such use must comply with applicable privacy and data protection requirements.

### 4.2 Restricted School Community Use

Selected media may be shared through restricted parent portals, class communications, or other authenticated school services. Access should be limited to appropriate recipients.

Restricted access does not authorise recipients to redistribute media publicly.

### 4.3 Public School Website Publication

Selected photographs, achievements, or other materials may be published on publicly accessible sections of the SRSS Digital Hub or official School website. Where required, appropriate consent must be obtained before publication.

### 4.4 Social Media Publication

The School may maintain official social media channels for institutional communications. Publication of identifiable pupil media through such channels requires particular consideration because third-party platforms may allow sharing, copying, downloading, or redistribution.

The School shall obtain appropriate authorisation where required and should minimise unnecessary identifying information.

### 4.5 Printed Publications

Media may be included in approved school magazines, newsletters, brochures, annual reports, anniversary publications, or other printed materials.

Permission for printed publication should be distinguished from permission for unrestricted online distribution where appropriate.

### 4.6 External Media and Collaborations

Media may occasionally be requested by external educational organisations, community partners, or media representatives.

Such use must be assessed separately and must not be presumed authorised merely because the material was previously used internally.

## 5. Parental and Guardian Consent

Where consent is required for the collection, use, or publication of a child’s media, the School shall obtain it from an appropriately authorised parent or legal guardian. Consent should be informed, appropriately documented, and sufficiently specific to the intended purpose.

The School shall endeavour to provide clear information concerning:

- The type of media involved.
- The intended purpose.
- The proposed audience.
- The publication channel.
- Whether the material may be publicly accessible.
- Whether external parties are involved.
- The relevant retention arrangements.
- Available withdrawal procedures.

### 5.1 Verification of Authority

The School may require reasonable verification of parental or guardianship authority. Where custody, guardianship, or access restrictions exist, the School may seek appropriate documentation before accepting or acting upon consent instructions.

### 5.2 Separate Consent Choices

Where practicable, parents should be offered distinct choices concerning:

- Internal educational documentation.
- Restricted parent or school-community sharing.
- Public website publication.
- Official social media publication.
- Printed promotional or commemorative publications.
- External media or educational collaborations.

The School should not treat consent to essential educational activities as blanket permission for unrelated publicity.

### 5.3 Refusal of Optional Consent

A parent’s refusal to authorise optional public media publication should not ordinarily prevent the child from participating in essential educational activities. The School may adopt reasonable practical arrangements to respect the refusal.

## 6. Digital Consent Records

The SRSS Digital Hub may progressively incorporate a digital consent management function. Where implemented, this function may record:

- Pupil identity.
- Parent or guardian identity.
- Consent category.
- Applicable consent wording or version.
- Date and time of authorisation.
- Consent status.
- Subsequent amendments.
- Withdrawal history.
- Relevant administrative verification.

Consent records shall be accessible only to authorised personnel.

The School may use electronic consent mechanisms where legally appropriate and sufficiently reliable for the relevant purpose. Where a particular activity requires additional verification or a specific form of authorisation, the School may require further documentation.

## 7. Pupil Dignity and Participation

Parental or guardian consent does not remove the School’s responsibility to consider the child’s dignity and welfare. Where appropriate to the child’s age and maturity, the School should explain the intended media activity and consider the child’s wishes.

A pupil should not be unnecessarily pressured to participate in optional promotional photography or recordings. The School should avoid media activities that may:

- Embarrass or humiliate a child.
- Expose sensitive personal circumstances.
- Cause unnecessary distress.
- Disclose confidential health or welfare information.
- Create foreseeable safeguarding risks.
- Present a child in a misleading or exploitative manner.

Where a child expresses significant discomfort with optional media publication, the School should review the proposed use even where parental consent has been provided.

## 8. Identification of Pupils in Published Media

The School shall endeavour to minimise unnecessary disclosure of identifying information. Depending on the circumstances, published media may identify pupils by first name, class, achievement, or other limited details where appropriate and authorised.

The School should avoid publicly disclosing:

- Full dates of birth.
- Identification numbers.
- Residential addresses.
- Personal telephone numbers.
- Private email addresses.
- Medical information.
- Sensitive family circumstances.
- Custody or safeguarding information.
- Other details that could unnecessarily expose a child to risk.

The combination of an image with identifying details must be assessed carefully. Where safeguarding restrictions apply, the School may prohibit publication even where general media consent exists.

## 9. Photography and Recording During School Activities

School activities may include classroom lessons, assemblies, competitions, performances, sports events, service activities, excursions, and celebrations. Official photography and recording should be undertaken by authorised personnel or approved service providers.

The School may establish specific rules for events involving children. These may include restrictions on recording, publication, live streaming, or sharing through external platforms.

### 9.1 Classroom Activities

Classroom media should be collected only for legitimate educational or authorised institutional purposes. Recording should not unnecessarily interfere with teaching or learning.

### 9.2 School Performances and Events

Where performances or events are recorded, the School should consider the number of children involved, the intended audience, and the practical limits of obtaining or implementing individual publication permissions.

Where necessary, alternative arrangements should be made for pupils whose media should not be publicly disclosed.

### 9.3 Excursions and External Activities

Media recorded during excursions or external programmes remains subject to applicable school policies. The involvement of an external venue or organiser does not automatically authorise unrestricted publication of pupil images.

## 10. Photography by Parents and Event Attendees

Parents and guardians may wish to photograph or record school events for personal or family purposes. Such activities may be permitted subject to event-specific instructions, the privacy of others, and applicable law.

Parents and attendees are expected to respect the dignity and privacy of other pupils and families. The School may restrict photography or recording at certain events, particularly where safeguarding, confidentiality, or child welfare considerations require additional protection.

### 10.1 Public Sharing by Parents

Parents should exercise caution before publishing photographs or recordings that identify children other than their own. Permission to attend or photograph a school event does not automatically constitute permission from other families for unrestricted public distribution.

The School may request that inappropriate or unauthorised publications be removed. However, the School does not necessarily control independently operated personal accounts or external platforms.

## 11. Photography and Recording by External Service Providers

The School may engage photographers, videographers, designers, printers, or other service providers for authorised activities. Where such providers process personal information or media involving children, appropriate confidentiality, security, use, and retention arrangements should be established.

External providers must not use pupil media for unrelated advertising, portfolios, commercial promotion, or other purposes without appropriate authorisation.

The School should ensure that relevant providers understand applicable safeguarding requirements.

## 12. Student Work and Creative Contributions

Pupil-created artwork, writing, performances, and other original works may be used to celebrate learning and creativity. The School recognises that such materials may be protected by intellectual property law.

Educational submission does not automatically transfer copyright ownership to the School or SSIOM.

### 12.1 Internal Display

Student work may be displayed within classrooms or restricted educational environments where lawful and appropriate.

### 12.2 Public Display

Public display or promotional use may require additional permission from the relevant rights holder and appropriate parental or guardian authorisation.

### 12.3 Attribution

Where appropriate, the School may acknowledge the pupil’s contribution. Attribution should be balanced against privacy and safeguarding considerations.

### 12.4 Commercial Exploitation

Student-created work must not be commercially exploited without appropriate legal authority and permission.

## 13. SRSS25 Silver Jubilee Media and Historical Archives

The SRSS25 initiative may involve the collection, digitisation, preservation, and publication of historical materials relating to the School’s development. Such materials may include:

- Historical school photographs.
- Former pupil photographs.
- School magazines.
- Anniversary publications.
- Alumni testimonials.
- Teacher reflections.
- Archival recordings.
- Historical event documentation.
- Other commemorative materials.

The School recognises that historical materials may involve individuals who are now adults, individuals whose contact information is unavailable, and materials subject to third-party copyright.

### 13.1 Historical Material Review

Before public publication, the School should consider:

- The source and ownership of the material.
- The age and context of the material.
- Whether identifiable individuals are depicted.
- The sensitivity of the circumstances.
- Available consent or other lawful grounds.
- Reasonable privacy expectations.
- Potential safeguarding concerns.
- Any relevant objections or restrictions.

### 13.2 Alumni Contributions

Alumni who voluntarily submit photographs, testimonials, or other materials should be informed of the intended use. Where necessary, appropriate permission should be obtained.

Submission does not automatically establish that the contributor owns all rights in the material.

### 13.3 Historical Photographs of Children

Historical photographs depicting children require careful consideration even where the individuals are now adults. The School should avoid assuming that age, historical significance, or prior publication automatically removes privacy or copyright considerations.

### 13.4 Removal Requests

Individuals may request review of historical material that identifies or concerns them. Requests shall be considered according to applicable law, the nature of the material, legitimate archival interests, and relevant privacy or safeguarding concerns.

## 14. Media Storage and Security

Media involving pupils shall be stored and managed through authorised systems. Appropriate safeguards may include:

- Restricted access.
- Approved cloud storage.
- Controlled sharing permissions.
- Secure transfer arrangements.
- Staff confidentiality obligations.
- Appropriate backup procedures.
- Retention and disposal controls.

Staff should avoid storing sensitive pupil media in unauthorised personal accounts or devices. Where temporary local storage is necessary for legitimate school activities, appropriate security and deletion procedures should apply.

## 15. Media Retention

Media shall be retained only for as long as reasonably necessary for its legitimate purpose, subject to applicable legal, educational, archival, and institutional requirements.

Different categories of media may require different retention arrangements. For example, temporary classroom recordings may have different retention needs from officially approved historical archives. The School should establish appropriate retention periods and procedures.

This Policy does not create an unrestricted right to retain or publish children’s media indefinitely.

## 16. Withdrawal of Consent

Parents or guardians may request withdrawal of consent for processing activities that depend on their consent. Requests should identify the pupil and the relevant media or publication category where reasonably possible.

Following receipt of a valid request, the School shall assess the applicable legal requirements and take appropriate action. This may include:

- Ceasing future optional publication.
- Updating consent records.
- Removing material from School-controlled online channels where required.
- Restricting further distribution.
- Notifying relevant authorised personnel.

### 16.1 Limitations of Withdrawal

Withdrawal does not necessarily invalidate processing lawfully undertaken before withdrawal. The School may also be unable to retrieve materials already distributed in printed publications or independently copied by third parties. Such limitations should be explained transparently.

Where the School controls a publication channel, it shall take reasonable steps required by applicable law to respond to a valid withdrawal or removal request.

## 17. Complaints and Media Removal Requests

Individuals who believe that media has been used inappropriately may contact the School. A request should, where possible, identify:

- The material concerned.
- The publication location.
- The reason for concern.
- The relationship of the requester to the person depicted.
- Relevant consent or safeguarding information.

The School shall review complaints according to their urgency and nature. Where a complaint raises a credible risk to a child’s safety, protective measures should be considered promptly rather than awaiting completion of an ordinary administrative review.

The School may temporarily restrict access to disputed material while assessing the matter.

## 18. Unauthorised Media Publication

Users must not upload, distribute, or publish pupil media obtained through restricted school systems without appropriate authorisation. This includes screenshots, recordings, downloaded photographs, and other materials obtained through parent, teacher, or pupil accounts.

Unauthorised publication may result in access restrictions, administrative action, disciplinary procedures, or referral to appropriate authorities where required. The School may request removal of material that infringes privacy, safeguarding, or intellectual property requirements.

## 19. Changes to This Policy

This Policy may be reviewed and amended to reflect changes in:

- Applicable law.
- Digital media technologies.
- Platform functionality.
- School activities.
- Safeguarding requirements.
- Consent management procedures.
- Institutional publication practices.

Material changes shall be communicated through appropriate channels. Where new media uses require additional consent or notice, the School shall undertake the necessary procedures.

## 20. Governing Law

This Policy shall be interpreted in accordance with the laws of Malaysia, including applicable copyright, privacy, child protection, and education-related legislation.

Nothing in this Policy overrides rights or obligations that cannot lawfully be excluded.
