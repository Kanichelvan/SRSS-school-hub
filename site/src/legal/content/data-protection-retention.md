## 1. Introduction

Sekolah Rendah Sathya Sai (SRSS) recognises that the responsible management of information is fundamental to maintaining the trust of pupils, parents, guardians, teachers, employees, and the wider school community.

As a primary school, SRSS is entrusted with information relating to children, their families, academic development, attendance, educational support, welfare, and other matters requiring appropriate confidentiality and care.

The SRSS Digital Hub is established under the auspices of the Sathya Sai International Organisation of Malaysia (SSIOM) to support the School’s progressive digital transformation. Its inaugural initiative, SRSS25, commemorates the School’s Silver Jubilee while introducing the foundations for a progressively integrated digital environment.

This Data Protection, Information Security & Records Retention Policy (“Policy”) establishes the principles governing the protection, management, security, retention, archival, and disposal of information processed through the Platform.

The Policy applies to authorised users, school personnel, administrators, technical contributors, service providers, and other individuals involved in handling information through the Platform.

It is intended to operate consistently with the Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Act 709), as amended, to the extent applicable, and relevant educational, employment, child protection, and other legal requirements.

The School recognises that information security involves not only technology but also responsible behaviour, clear institutional accountability, appropriate access controls, and effective administrative procedures.

## 2. Institutional Responsibilities

### 2.1 Sathya Sai International Organisation of Malaysia

SSIOM is the parent organisation associated with SRSS and the designated owner of the Platform’s original software and proprietary digital infrastructure, subject to applicable rights and development arrangements. SSIOM may provide authorised technical development, maintenance, and infrastructure support.

Ownership of Platform software does not automatically establish SSIOM as the sole party responsible for all personal information processed through the Platform.

### 2.2 Sekolah Rendah Sathya Berhad

Sekolah Rendah Sathya Berhad (Registration No. 263453-U) is the registered governing entity responsible for SRSS. It provides the institutional governance structure through which the School undertakes its educational and administrative responsibilities.

### 2.3 Sekolah Rendah Sathya Sai

SRSS manages the day-to-day educational and administrative use of the Platform through its Principal and authorised personnel. The School is responsible for ensuring that its personnel follow applicable information handling procedures.

### 2.4 Principal

The Principal serves as the designated operational contact for Platform administration and related information management matters. The School has appointed its Senior Assistant for Student Affairs as Data Protection Officer, supported by a Deputy Data Protection Officer (the School Data Teacher). The Data Protection Officer handles day-to-day data protection matters under the Principal’s supervision.

Where applicable law requires the appointment of a Data Protection Officer to be notified to or registered with the Personal Data Protection Commissioner, or requires any other designated compliance function, the responsible entity shall do so.

### 2.5 Technical Personnel

Authorised technical personnel may provide maintenance, troubleshooting, security, and development support. Their access must be limited to legitimate responsibilities and governed by appropriate confidentiality and security arrangements.

## 4. Information Classification

The School may classify information according to its sensitivity and the potential consequences of unauthorised disclosure.

### 4.1 Public Information

Information approved for public access, such as general school announcements, admissions information, and published institutional materials.

### 4.2 Internal Information

Information intended for authorised school personnel or defined school-community groups.

### 4.3 Confidential Information

Information requiring restricted access, including individual pupil records, staff information, private parent communications, and certain administrative documents.

### 4.4 Highly Sensitive Information

Information requiring enhanced safeguards, including safeguarding reports, medical information, sensitive welfare records, custody restrictions, and other particularly sensitive personal data.

The School should apply access restrictions and handling procedures appropriate to each classification.

## 5. Data Protection Principles

Information processed through the Platform should be managed according to the following principles.

### 5.1 Lawfulness

Personal information must be processed in accordance with applicable legal requirements.

### 5.2 Purpose Limitation

Information should be collected and used for specified, legitimate purposes.

### 5.3 Data Minimisation

Only information reasonably necessary for the relevant purpose should be collected.

### 5.4 Accuracy

Reasonable steps should be taken to maintain accurate and current information.

### 5.5 Security

Appropriate safeguards should be implemented against unauthorised access, loss, misuse, alteration, or disclosure.

### 5.6 Retention Limitation

Information should not be retained longer than reasonably necessary, subject to applicable legal and institutional requirements.

### 5.7 Accountability

Responsibilities for information management should be appropriately defined and documented.

## 6. Role-Based Access Control

The Platform may implement role-based access arrangements. Access privileges should be assigned according to a user’s verified identity and legitimate responsibilities. Examples may include:

- Pupils accessing their own approved learning information.
- Parents accessing records concerning their authorised children.
- Teachers accessing information necessary for assigned teaching responsibilities.
- Administrative staff accessing relevant operational records.
- School leadership accessing information required for institutional oversight.
- Technical administrators accessing systems necessary for authorised maintenance.

Access to highly sensitive information should be subject to additional restrictions. No individual should receive unrestricted access solely because of organisational seniority or technical capability.

## 7. Account Security

Users must protect their account credentials and comply with applicable security requirements. The School may implement:

- Individual user accounts.
- Secure authentication.
- Password requirements.
- Multi-factor authentication where appropriate.
- Session expiration controls.
- Account recovery procedures.
- Restricted administrative privileges.
- Access reviews.

Shared administrative accounts should be avoided where individual accountability is reasonably achievable. Users should promptly report suspected unauthorised access.

## 8. Privileged and Technical Access

Technical administrators may require elevated privileges for maintenance and support. Such access should be limited to authorised functions. Where reasonably practicable, the School should implement:

- Named administrative accounts.
- Privileged access logging.
- Restricted production access.
- Approval procedures for sensitive operations.
- Separation of development and live environments.
- Periodic permission reviews.
- Prompt revocation of unnecessary privileges.

Technical support personnel must not access confidential pupil information for curiosity, personal interest, or unrelated purposes.

## 9. Secure Storage and Transmission

The School shall endeavour to use appropriate safeguards for information stored or transmitted through the Platform. These may include:

- Encrypted connections.
- Secure cloud storage.
- Restricted database access.
- Appropriate encryption of sensitive information.
- Controlled file-sharing permissions.
- Secure backup arrangements.
- Appropriate system configuration.
- Regular security updates.

The specific controls implemented shall depend on the Platform’s technical architecture, the nature of the information, and available resources.

The School should assess whether additional protections are required before introducing functions involving highly sensitive records.

## 10. Cloud Services and External Providers

The Platform may use approved cloud infrastructure, hosting services, Google Workspace for Education, and other authorised technology providers. The responsible entity shall assess relevant contractual, security, confidentiality, and data protection requirements.

Where external providers process personal information on behalf of the School or governing entity, their responsibilities should be appropriately documented. Relevant considerations may include:

- Permitted processing purposes.
- Access restrictions.
- Confidentiality.
- Security measures.
- Data location.
- Subcontracting arrangements.
- Incident notification.
- Data return or deletion.
- Service termination procedures.

The use of a third-party provider does not automatically remove the responsible entity’s applicable legal obligations. The providers currently in use are listed in [Schedule A of the Cookies, Analytics & Digital Technologies Policy](/legal/cookies#schedule-a-technologies-in-use).

## 11. Cross-Border Data Transfers

Certain technology providers may process or store information outside Malaysia. Where personal information is transferred internationally, the responsible entity shall comply with applicable Malaysian data protection requirements.

The School should assess relevant transfer conditions, contractual protections, security arrangements, and the sensitivity of the information. Additional consideration should be given to transfers involving children’s information, medical records, or safeguarding documentation.

## 12. Information Security Monitoring

The School may maintain technical logs and security monitoring arrangements to support legitimate operational purposes. These may include monitoring for:

- Unauthorised login attempts.
- Suspicious access activity.
- System errors.
- Security vulnerabilities.
- Unexpected configuration changes.
- Unauthorised data exports.
- Other relevant security events.

Monitoring must be proportionate and consistent with applicable privacy requirements. Security monitoring must not be used as a pretext for unjustified surveillance of pupils, parents, or employees.

## 13. Security Incidents and Personal Data Breaches

A security incident may involve unauthorised access, malicious activity, system compromise, data loss, or other events affecting the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information. A personal data breach may involve unauthorised disclosure, access, loss, alteration, or other compromise of personal information.

Suspected incidents should be reported promptly to the designated operational contact. The School should maintain appropriate procedures for:

- Receiving incident reports.
- Assessing the nature and seriousness of the incident.
- Containing ongoing risks.
- Preserving relevant evidence.
- Identifying affected systems and information.
- Implementing corrective measures.
- Determining applicable notification obligations.
- Documenting actions and outcomes.
- Reviewing preventive measures.

Where statutory notification requirements apply, the responsible entity shall comply with the applicable criteria and timelines. Incidents involving children’s information may require additional safeguarding responses.

## 14. Data Backup and Recovery

The School recognises the importance of maintaining appropriate backup and recovery arrangements for essential records. Where implemented, backup procedures may address:

- Frequency of backups.
- Storage locations.
- Access restrictions.
- Backup encryption.
- Restoration procedures.
- Recovery testing.
- Retention periods.
- Secure disposal.

Backup arrangements should reflect the importance of the information and the operational consequences of its loss.

The School should not assume that a cloud service automatically provides every backup or recovery capability required for its educational records.

## 15. Records Retention Principles

The School shall endeavour to retain information only for as long as reasonably necessary for legitimate educational, administrative, legal, safeguarding, or archival purposes. Retention periods may differ according to the type of information.

The School should establish an approved records retention schedule before implementing automated deletion or archival processes. Retention decisions should consider:

- Applicable legal obligations.
- Ministry of Education requirements.
- Educational recordkeeping needs.
- Safeguarding responsibilities.
- Financial and accounting obligations.
- Employment requirements.
- Dispute resolution needs.
- Legitimate historical and archival interests.

No single retention period should be applied indiscriminately to all categories of information.

The retention periods currently approved by the School are set out in [Schedule A](#schedule-a-records-retention-periods).

## 16. Pupil Academic Records

Academic records may include:

- Enrolment details.
- Class histories.
- Attendance.
- Assessment results.
- Academic reports.
- Learning portfolios.
- Educational achievements.
- Other relevant school records.

Certain records may require longer retention due to educational, administrative, or legal purposes. The School should distinguish between official academic records, working documents, temporary submissions, and historical archives.

Where pupils leave or complete their education at SRSS, access privileges may be adjusted while relevant records are retained according to approved procedures.

## 17. Safeguarding and Welfare Records

Safeguarding and welfare records require particular care. Such records may contain highly sensitive information concerning children and families. They should be subject to:

- Strict access restrictions.
- Appropriate confidentiality.
- Secure storage.
- Controlled disclosure.
- Documented handling procedures.
- Legally appropriate retention arrangements.

Safeguarding records should not be automatically deleted merely because a pupil leaves the School. Retention and disposal decisions must consider applicable legal obligations, potential investigations, and the continuing interests of the child.

## 18. Parental Consent Records

Consent records may be retained to demonstrate the nature and scope of authorisation provided. Such records may include:

- Identity of the consenting person.
- Relevant pupil information.
- Consent purpose.
- Applicable consent wording.
- Date and time.
- Consent status.
- Withdrawal or amendment history.
- Relevant verification information.

The School may retain necessary evidence of consent or withdrawal where justified by applicable legal or administrative requirements. Consent records should not be used for unrelated purposes.

## 19. Financial and Administrative Records

The Platform may progressively support school-related financial and administrative functions. Relevant records may include:

- Fee-related information.
- Payment references.
- Receipts.
- Administrative correspondence.
- Financial assistance information.
- Other institutional records.

Such information must be handled according to applicable accounting, taxation, privacy, and institutional requirements. Access should be restricted to authorised personnel.

Sensitive financial information should not be displayed through general pupil or parent interfaces unless necessary and appropriately authorised.

## 20. Staff and Employment Records

Staff records may be processed for legitimate employment and administrative purposes. These may include employment information, professional qualifications, assigned duties, access permissions, and other relevant records.

Retention and access arrangements shall be determined according to applicable employment, privacy, and institutional requirements.

The termination of employment should trigger appropriate review and revocation of system access. Records required for lawful purposes may be retained after employment ends.

## 21. Alumni and Historical Records

SRSS may maintain selected historical information concerning former pupils, teachers, and school activities. Such records may support legitimate archival, commemorative, or institutional purposes. The SRSS25 initiative may involve digitisation and preservation of historical materials.

The School should distinguish between records required for ongoing administration and materials retained for legitimate historical purposes.

Public publication of identifiable historical information remains subject to applicable privacy, consent, intellectual property, and safeguarding considerations. Alumni participation in optional digital services should be governed by appropriate notices and permissions.

## 22. Data Archival

Records no longer required for active daily operations may be transferred to restricted archival storage where continued retention is justified. Archived records should remain subject to appropriate access controls and security measures.

Archival status does not automatically authorise public disclosure.

The School should establish procedures for retrieval, correction, preservation, and disposal of archived information where appropriate.

## 23. Secure Disposal and Deletion

Where information is no longer required and no lawful retention obligation applies, it should be securely deleted, anonymised, or otherwise disposed of. Disposal procedures should reflect the sensitivity and format of the information. These may include:

- Secure deletion of electronic files.
- Removal of unnecessary account data.
- Disposal of obsolete storage media.
- Secure destruction of printed records.
- Review of relevant backup retention.
- Appropriate instructions to service providers.

The School should maintain suitable records of significant disposal activities where necessary.

## 24. Requests for Access, Correction and Other Rights

Individuals may submit requests concerning their personal information in accordance with applicable Malaysian law. Requests may involve:

- Access to personal information.
- Correction of inaccurate information.
- Withdrawal of consent where applicable.
- Other rights recognised by relevant legislation.

The School may require reasonable identity verification before disclosing or amending confidential records. Where requests concern children, the School may require verification of parental or guardianship authority.

Requests shall be assessed according to applicable legal requirements, statutory exceptions, and legitimate safeguarding considerations.

## 25. Data Portability and Transfers Between Schools

Where applicable law or authorised educational procedures permit, certain pupil records may be transferred to another educational institution. Such transfers should be limited to information legitimately required for the relevant purpose.

The School should verify the identity and authority of the receiving party. Where appropriate, secure transmission methods should be used.

The Platform does not automatically provide unrestricted data portability or bulk export rights beyond those required by applicable law or authorised school procedures.

## 26. Use of Artificial Intelligence and Automated Technologies

The School may consider introducing artificial intelligence or automated technologies as part of future digital development. Any such introduction should be subject to appropriate review. Relevant considerations include:

- Educational necessity.
- Accuracy and reliability.
- Children’s privacy.
- Safeguarding.
- Human oversight.
- Bias and fairness.
- Data security.
- Third-party processing.
- Appropriate parental information or consent where required.

Confidential pupil information should not be entered into unauthorised public artificial intelligence services.

Automated outputs should not be treated as definitive judgments concerning a pupil’s academic ability, behaviour, welfare, or character. Decisions with significant consequences for pupils should remain subject to appropriate human review and applicable legal requirements.

This clause does not imply that artificial intelligence functions are currently active within the Platform.

## 27. Staff Training and Confidentiality

Staff and authorised personnel should receive appropriate guidance concerning information security and confidentiality. Training may address:

- Password and account security.
- Appropriate handling of pupil records.
- Confidentiality obligations.
- Phishing awareness.
- Secure communications.
- Incident reporting.
- Safeguarding information.
- Third-party applications.
- Responsible use of digital tools.

The School should review training needs as Platform functions expand.

Confidentiality obligations may continue after a person’s employment or authorised involvement ends, subject to applicable law and agreements.

## 28. Periodic Review of Security Controls

The School may conduct periodic reviews of information security arrangements. Reviews may consider:

- Access permissions.
- Administrative accounts.
- Security updates.
- Cloud configurations.
- Backup arrangements.
- Incident records.
- Third-party integrations.
- Data retention practices.
- Staff awareness.
- Emerging risks.

Where material weaknesses are identified, the School should consider proportionate corrective measures. The frequency and scope of reviews should reflect the Platform’s operational risks and available resources.

## 29. Responsibilities of Users

All users share responsibility for protecting information accessed through the Platform. Users must:

- Protect account credentials.
- Access only authorised information.
- Avoid sharing confidential records.
- Report suspected security incidents.
- Follow approved communication procedures.
- Respect other users’ privacy.
- Avoid unauthorised downloads or data exports.
- Use school information only for legitimate purposes.

Users must not attempt to circumvent access restrictions or obtain information concerning other pupils or families without authorisation.

## 30. Policy Compliance

Breaches of this Policy may result in appropriate administrative, technical, disciplinary, contractual, or legal action. Responses should reflect the seriousness of the incident and applicable legal requirements.

Where a breach involves a child, the School should consider age, maturity, safeguarding needs, and appropriate educational responses.

Where misconduct involves an employee, administrator, service provider, or other adult, relevant employment, contractual, safeguarding, or legal procedures may apply.

## 31. Changes to This Policy

This Policy may be revised to reflect changes in:

- Educational recordkeeping requirements.
- Platform functionality.
- Information security practices.
- Third-party services.

## 32. Governing Law

This Policy shall be governed by and interpreted in accordance with the laws of Malaysia. Relevant legislation may include the Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Act 709), as amended, the Education Act 1996 (Act 550), and other applicable laws.

Nothing in this Policy excludes or limits obligations or rights that cannot lawfully be excluded.

## Schedule A — Records Retention Periods

The following retention periods apply to the School’s records, whether held on paper or in the SRSS Digital Hub. A record may be kept longer where it is needed for an active inquiry, legal claim, police investigation or other legal requirement. When a period ends, the record is securely deleted or destroyed in accordance with section 23.

### A.1 Pupil Records

| Record | Retention period | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| General student files, transcripts and enrolment registers | 7 years after the pupil leaves or completes their education at SRSS | The limitation period for civil claims under the Limitation Act 1953, and standard practice for private schools under the Ministry of Education |
| Disciplinary and behavioural records | 7 years after the pupil leaves the School | The limitation period for civil claims |
| Medical and health information | Archived for 7 years after the pupil leaves the School | The limitation period for civil claims in Malaysia |

### A.2 Child Protection and Safeguarding Records

| Record | Retention period | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Child protection and safeguarding records | Until the pupil reaches 25 years of age | School risk management. Kept separate from other pupil records, confidential, and under restricted access |
| Records of sexual abuse or other serious allegations (concerning staff or pupils) | For the duration of any active inquiry, or for at least 30 years or until the individual concerned reaches normal retirement age, whichever is longer | Protection against historical institutional liability |
| Staff safeguarding training records | 7 years after the staff member leaves the School | Consistent with other employment records |

### A.3 Staff and Human Resources Records

| Record | Retention period | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Personnel files, employment contracts, and disciplinary and grievance records | At least 7 years after employment ends | The 6-year limitation period under the Limitation Act 1953, plus one year |
| Background vetting records (PDRM / e-SGP) | 7 years after employment ends, as part of the personnel file | Consistent with the personnel file |
| Payroll, tax and statutory contribution records (EPF, SOCSO, EIS, PCB) | 7 years from the end of the relevant year of assessment | Requirements of the Inland Revenue Board of Malaysia (LHDN) and Malaysian employment law |

### A.4 Operational Records

| Record | Retention period | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| CCTV footage | 60 days, after which it is automatically overwritten | Unless the footage is flagged for an active security incident or police investigation |
