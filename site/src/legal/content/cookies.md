## 1. Introduction

The SRSS Digital Hub (“the Site”, “the Platform”, “the Hub”, “we”, “our”, or “us”, as applicable) is an evolving digital platform established under the auspices of the Sathya Sai International Organisation of Malaysia (SSIOM) to support the educational, administrative, and community activities of Sekolah Rendah Sathya Sai (SRSS).

The Platform is developed as part of the School’s progressive digital transformation, commencing with SRSS25, its inaugural Silver Jubilee initiative, and is intended to provide a secure, accessible, and integrated digital environment for pupils, parents, guardians, teachers, school administrators, and authorised members of the school community.

This Cookies, Analytics & Digital Technologies Policy (“Policy”) explains how cookies, browser storage, authentication technologies, analytics tools, embedded content, and other digital technologies may be used in connection with the Platform. The Policy also explains the purposes of such technologies, the information they may collect, the involvement of third-party service providers, and the choices available to users.

SRSS recognises that digital technologies must be implemented responsibly, particularly where their use involves children and educational records. Accordingly, the School shall endeavour to adopt technologies that are appropriate to its educational objectives, proportionate to its operational needs, and consistent with applicable Malaysian privacy and data protection requirements.

This Policy should be read together with the [Terms of Use](/legal/terms), the [Privacy Policy](/legal/privacy), the [Child Protection & Online Safeguarding Policy](/legal/online-safeguarding), and other applicable SRSS Digital Hub policies.

## 2. What Are Cookies?

Cookies are small text files that websites may place on a user’s device through a web browser. They may allow a website to recognise a browser, maintain a login session, remember preferences, or support other technical functions.

Cookies do not necessarily identify a person by name. However, certain cookies and associated identifiers may constitute personal data where they identify or can reasonably be linked to an individual.

The Platform may also use related technologies, including browser local storage, session storage, authentication tokens, and other mechanisms necessary for authorised digital services. These technologies may be introduced or modified as the Platform develops.

## 3. Categories of Cookies and Digital Technologies

The Platform may use the following categories, subject to actual technical implementation. The technologies actually in use are listed in [Schedule A](#schedule-a-technologies-in-use).

### 3.1 Strictly Necessary Cookies

Strictly necessary cookies support functions required for the Platform to operate. These may include:

- User authentication.
- Login session management.
- Security verification.
- Prevention of unauthorised access.
- Session continuity.
- Protection against certain malicious requests.
- Maintenance of essential user settings.

Disabling these technologies may prevent some Platform functions from operating correctly.

### 3.2 Functional Cookies

Functional cookies may remember selected user preferences, such as:

- Language preferences.
- Display settings.
- Accessibility preferences.
- Previously selected interface options.
- Other non-essential personalisation settings.

Where such technologies are optional, the School shall determine appropriate notice and choice mechanisms.

### 3.3 Performance and Diagnostic Technologies

The Platform may use technical monitoring tools to identify errors, assess performance, and maintain service reliability. Such tools may collect information concerning:

- Page loading times.
- System errors.
- Browser compatibility.
- Device characteristics.
- Service availability.
- Technical response times.
- Application failures.

The School should minimise personal information collected for these purposes and restrict access to authorised technical personnel.

### 3.4 Analytics Technologies

The School may introduce analytics tools to understand general Platform usage and improve digital services. Analytics may involve information such as:

- Number of visits.
- Frequently accessed pages.
- General usage patterns.
- Browser or device categories.
- Referral sources.
- Technical performance information.

Where practicable, the School should use aggregated or appropriately de-identified information. Analytics involving identifiable pupils must be subject to additional privacy and safeguarding considerations.

### 3.5 Third-Party Technologies

Certain third-party services may use their own cookies, authentication mechanisms, or related technologies. Such services may include approved educational applications, cloud platforms, embedded videos, and other authorised integrations.

Their use shall be subject to relevant provider terms and the School’s applicable privacy and safeguarding obligations.

## 4. Children’s Privacy and Digital Tracking

The SRSS Digital Hub serves primary-school pupils and must therefore exercise particular care when implementing tracking and analytics technologies. The School shall endeavour to ensure that technologies used within pupil-facing services are appropriate for their educational purpose.

Children’s information must not be collected or processed for unrelated behavioural advertising or commercial profiling through School-controlled services without an appropriate legal basis and institutional approval.

The School should avoid introducing unnecessary third-party tracking technologies into authenticated pupil environments. Where analytics are required for educational or operational purposes, access and processing should be restricted to the information reasonably necessary.

The School shall not treat general acceptance of website cookies as blanket parental consent for unrelated processing of children’s personal information.

## 5. Authentication and Session Management

Restricted areas of the Platform may require users to log in through school-managed accounts or approved authentication services. Authentication technologies may be used to:

- Verify user identity.
- Maintain secure sessions.
- Enforce access permissions.
- Detect suspicious activity.
- Support account security.
- Reduce unauthorised access.

Users are responsible for protecting their credentials and logging out from shared devices where appropriate.

The School may introduce additional authentication requirements for accounts with access to sensitive pupil or administrative information. Authentication records must be handled according to applicable privacy and retention requirements.

## 6. Google Workspace for Education

The Platform may integrate with Google Workspace for Education and associated services. These may include Google Classroom, Google Drive, Google Forms, Google Calendar, Google Meet, Gmail, and other approved applications.

Such services may use cookies or related technologies to provide authentication, document management, collaboration, communication, and other functions. The School shall endeavour to review and configure these services appropriately for educational use.

Users should refer to the relevant provider’s privacy documentation for further information concerning technologies independently operated by that provider. The School shall not assume that all third-party tracking or processing is controlled solely by the SRSS Digital Hub.

## 7. Embedded Content and External Websites

The Platform may display or link to externally hosted materials. Examples may include educational videos, maps, documents, forms, and other approved resources.

Embedded content may cause a user’s browser to communicate directly with a third-party service. Depending on the provider and configuration, such interactions may involve cookies or other technical identifiers.

Where reasonably practicable, the School should minimise unnecessary third-party tracking and provide appropriate notice concerning external services.

Links to external websites do not automatically indicate that the School controls those websites or their independent privacy practices.

## 8. Consent and User Choices

Where applicable law requires consent for particular processing activities, the responsible entity shall obtain appropriate consent before undertaking such processing.

The School may provide cookie settings, notices, or other mechanisms to allow users to manage optional technologies. Consent mechanisms should distinguish between technologies necessary for Platform operation and those used for optional purposes. Where consent is relied upon, withdrawal should be supported in accordance with applicable law.

The School shall not imply that a user has consented merely because a browser technically accepts cookies. For pupil accounts, parental or guardian authorisation shall be obtained where legally required.

## 9. Browser Controls

Most modern browsers allow users to manage or delete cookies. Users may be able to:

- View stored cookies.
- Delete existing cookies.
- Block certain cookies.
- Restrict third-party cookies.
- Clear browser storage.
- Adjust privacy settings.

Disabling essential technologies may affect authentication, account access, or other Platform functions. Browser settings may not control all forms of server-side processing or third-party technologies.

## 10. Data Collected Through Digital Technologies

Depending on the functions implemented, the Platform may process technical information including:

- IP addresses.
- Device identifiers.
- Browser types.
- Operating system information.
- Access timestamps.
- Session identifiers.
- Authentication activity.
- Error logs.
- Security events.
- General usage information.

Where such information constitutes personal data, it shall be processed in accordance with the [Privacy Policy](/legal/privacy) and applicable law. Technical information must not be retained indefinitely without a legitimate purpose.

## 11. Use of Analytics Information

Analytics information may be used to:

- Improve Platform usability.
- Identify technical problems.
- Evaluate service performance.
- Understand general adoption of digital services.
- Support capacity planning.
- Improve accessibility.
- Strengthen information security.
- Guide future Platform development.

Analytics should not be used to make unjustified judgments concerning a child’s character, behaviour, academic potential, or personal worth.

Where educational analytics involving identifiable pupils are introduced, the School should establish appropriate purposes, access restrictions, safeguards, and review procedures.

## 12. Advertising and Commercial Tracking

The SRSS Digital Hub is established primarily for educational and institutional purposes. The School does not intend to use its authenticated pupil environment as a platform for unrelated targeted commercial advertising.

The School should not permit unauthorised advertising networks or data brokers to collect children’s information through School-controlled digital services.

Any proposed commercial technology integration must undergo appropriate institutional, privacy, and safeguarding review before implementation.

## 13. Data Sharing with Technology Providers

Technical information may be processed by authorised service providers where necessary to deliver Platform functions. Such providers may include:

- Hosting services.
- Cloud infrastructure providers.
- Authentication providers.
- Security monitoring services.
- Approved educational technology providers.
- Technical maintenance providers.

The responsible entity shall assess relevant privacy, security, confidentiality, and contractual requirements. Technical providers must not be granted broader access to pupil information than reasonably necessary for their authorised functions.

## 14. Cross-Border Processing

Certain cloud services or digital technologies may involve processing or storage outside Malaysia. Where personal data is transferred internationally, the responsible entity shall comply with applicable Malaysian legal requirements concerning cross-border transfers.

The School should assess relevant contractual protections, service configurations, and the nature of the information involved. Additional safeguards may be appropriate where information concerns children or sensitive educational records.

## 15. Retention of Technical Information

Cookies and related technologies may have different durations. Some operate only during a browser session, while others may remain for a specified period. Technical logs and analytics information may also be retained for different periods according to operational requirements.

The School shall endeavour to retain identifiable information only for as long as reasonably necessary, subject to applicable legal obligations.

The names, providers, purposes, and durations of the technologies currently in use are listed in [Schedule A](#schedule-a-technologies-in-use). Schedule A will be reviewed whenever a technology is added, changed, or removed.

## 16. Security of Cookies and Authentication Technologies

The School shall endeavour to implement reasonable safeguards appropriate to the technologies used. These may include secure cookie attributes, encrypted connections, restricted session access, session expiration controls, and other suitable technical measures.

The precise safeguards implemented shall depend on the Platform’s architecture and the services activated.

Users should avoid sharing authentication credentials and should exercise caution when accessing restricted school services through public or shared devices.

## 17. Changes to Technologies

The Platform may introduce new technologies as part of its phased development. The School shall review the privacy and safeguarding implications of material changes before implementation.

Where new technologies require additional notice or consent, appropriate procedures shall be undertaken. The Policy may be updated to reflect such changes.

## 18. Changes to This Policy

This Policy may be revised to reflect developments in applicable law, technology, service providers, and institutional requirements.

Material changes shall be communicated through appropriate channels. The effective date and last revised date will be displayed on this page.

## 19. Governing Law

This Policy shall be interpreted in accordance with the laws of Malaysia, including the Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Act 709), as amended, to the extent applicable.

This schedule lists the cookies, browser storage and outside services the SRSS Digital Hub actually uses. It was prepared from a review of the Platform’s software on 13th October 2026 and will be confirmed again when the Platform goes live.

**No analytics, advertising or tracking tools are in use.** The Platform does not use Google Analytics, advertising networks, social media pixels or similar technologies. The School will update this schedule, and provide any required notice or choice, before introducing one.
