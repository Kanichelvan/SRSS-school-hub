## 1. Introduction

Sekolah Rendah Sathya Sai (SRSS) is committed to providing an educational environment that respects the dignity, individuality, and potential of every child. Guided by the philosophy of Sathya Sai Educare and the five universal Human Values of Truth, Right Conduct, Peace, Love, and Nonviolence, the School seeks to promote fairness, inclusivity, responsible conduct, and compassionate service.

The SRSS Digital Hub is established under the auspices of the Sathya Sai International Organisation of Malaysia (SSIOM) to support the School’s educational, administrative, and community activities. Its inaugural development initiative, SRSS25, commemorates the School’s Silver Jubilee while establishing the foundations for progressively accessible and integrated digital services.

This Accessibility, Complaints & Digital Governance Policy (“Policy”) outlines the School’s approach to digital accessibility, user assistance, complaints, institutional oversight, accountability, and responsible Platform administration. The Policy applies to pupils, parents, guardians, teachers, employees, administrators, volunteers, alumni, visitors, and other authorised users.

It recognises that the introduction of digital technology must not unnecessarily disadvantage individuals who experience disability-related barriers, limited digital literacy, connectivity difficulties, or other circumstances affecting access.

The Policy also establishes principles for handling concerns fairly and ensuring that the Platform remains accountable to its educational purpose.

## 2. Institutional Governance Structure

The SRSS Digital Hub operates within the institutional relationship between SSIOM, Sekolah Rendah Sathya Berhad, and SRSS.

### 2.1 Sathya Sai International Organisation of Malaysia

SSIOM serves as the parent organisation associated with SRSS and is the designated owner of the Platform’s original software and proprietary digital infrastructure, subject to applicable intellectual property arrangements.

SSIOM may provide authorised technical development, maintenance, and strategic support.

### 2.2 Sekolah Rendah Sathya Berhad

Sekolah Rendah Sathya Berhad (Registration No. 263453-U) is the registered entity responsible for the governance of SRSS. Its Board of Governors oversees institutional governance, administration, and financial stewardship.

### 2.3 Sekolah Rendah Sathya Sai

SRSS is a private primary school registered with the Ministry of Education Malaysia under the Education Act 1996 (Act 550), bearing SMIPS Code WDA0001.

The School is responsible for the day-to-day educational and administrative operation of the Platform through the Principal and authorised personnel.

### 2.4 Principal

The Principal serves as the designated operational person-in-charge for the SRSS Digital Hub. The Principal may coordinate Platform administration, user concerns, access issues, and relevant institutional responses.

The Principal may delegate specific responsibilities to suitably authorised personnel.

### 2.5 Parent-Teacher Association

The Parent-Teacher Association (PTA) may support school-community engagement, communication, feedback, and approved initiatives.

Participation in the PTA does not automatically confer administrative authority over the Platform or unrestricted access to pupil information.

## 3. Commitment to Digital Accessibility

SRSS recognises that digital accessibility is an important component of inclusive education. The School shall endeavour to make the Platform reasonably accessible and usable by individuals with different needs, abilities, devices, and levels of digital familiarity.

Accessibility considerations may include:

- Clear navigation.
- Readable text.
- Appropriate colour contrast.
- Keyboard accessibility.
- Screen-reader compatibility.
- Clear form labels.
- Understandable instructions.
- Responsive layouts.
- Accessible documents.
- Alternative communication arrangements.

The School may progressively evaluate its Platform against recognised accessibility guidance, including the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), where appropriate and practicable.

This Policy does not represent a certification that the Platform currently satisfies a particular WCAG conformance level.

## 4. Accessibility for Pupils

The Platform serves primary-school pupils of different ages and learning abilities. Digital services should be designed with consideration for children’s developmental needs.

Where appropriate, the School may provide:

- Simplified instructions.
- Age-appropriate interfaces.
- Clear visual navigation.
- Teacher-assisted access.
- Alternative learning formats.
- Reasonable accommodations for pupils with disabilities.
- Additional guidance for younger pupils.

The School should avoid requiring children to independently complete complex administrative or contractual processes. Where parental or guardian authorisation is required, appropriate adult involvement must be provided.

## 5. Accessibility for Parents and Guardians

Parents and guardians may have different levels of access to devices, internet connectivity, digital skills, and assistive technologies. The School recognises that digitalisation should support families rather than create unnecessary barriers to essential school services.

Where reasonably practicable, alternative arrangements may be provided for individuals unable to access particular digital functions. These may include:

- Assistance from authorised school personnel.
- Printed forms.
- Direct school communications.
- Alternative submission procedures.
- In-person administrative assistance.
- Other reasonable arrangements.

The availability of specific alternatives may depend on the nature of the service and the School’s operational capacity.

## 6. Accessibility Limitations

The School shall endeavour to identify and address accessibility barriers as the Platform develops. However, certain functions may depend on third-party technologies, legacy documents, or technical services that have their own limitations.

Where an accessibility issue prevents a user from obtaining essential information or completing an important school process, the user is encouraged to contact the School. The School shall consider reasonable alternatives and corrective measures according to the circumstances.

Accessibility improvements may be prioritised based on impact, urgency, feasibility, and available resources.

## 7. Reporting Accessibility Issues

Users may report difficulties involving:

- Inaccessible forms.
- Unreadable text.
- Navigation problems.
- Screen-reader incompatibility.
- Keyboard access limitations.
- Inaccessible documents.
- Mobile display problems.
- Other barriers affecting Platform use.

Reports should, where possible, identify the affected page or function and describe the difficulty encountered.

Users are not required to disclose unnecessary medical or disability-related information merely to report an accessibility issue. Where supporting information is needed to arrange an accommodation, it shall be handled confidentially and proportionately.

## 8. Complaints and Feedback

SRSS recognises that constructive feedback and accessible complaint procedures contribute to responsible institutional governance. Users may raise concerns relating to:

- Platform access.
- Technical errors.
- Inaccurate information.
- Account permissions.
- Privacy and confidentiality.
- Inappropriate digital content.
- Accessibility barriers.
- Unauthorised publication of media.
- Suspected misuse of Platform services.
- Other matters connected with the operation of the Hub.

Complaints should be handled respectfully, fairly, and with appropriate confidentiality.

## 9. Submission of Complaints

Complaints may be submitted through the School’s designated contact channels. Where possible, users should provide sufficient information to allow the School to understand and assess the concern. This may include:

- The nature of the complaint.
- The relevant Platform function.
- The date or approximate time of the incident.
- A description of the issue.
- Supporting information where appropriate.
- Contact details for follow-up.

The School should avoid requiring unnecessary personal information.

Complaints involving children or sensitive information should not be submitted through publicly visible discussion areas.

## 10. Complaint Handling Principles

The School shall endeavour to handle complaints according to the following principles.

### 10.1 Fairness

Complaints should be considered objectively and without unjustified prejudice.

### 10.2 Confidentiality

Information should be shared only with individuals who have a legitimate need to know or where disclosure is legally required.

### 10.3 Proportionality

The response should reflect the seriousness, urgency, and circumstances of the complaint.

### 10.4 Timeliness

Complaints should be addressed within a reasonable period according to their nature and complexity.

### 10.5 Accountability

Relevant decisions and actions should be appropriately documented.

### 10.6 Protection Against Retaliation

Individuals who raise genuine concerns in good faith should not be subjected to retaliation for doing so.

## 11. Complaint Review Process

Where appropriate, complaints may be addressed through the following process.

#### Stage 1 — Receipt and Acknowledgement

The School receives the complaint and identifies the relevant subject matter.

#### Stage 2 — Preliminary Assessment

The concern is assessed to determine its urgency, appropriate handling personnel, and whether immediate protective measures are necessary.

#### Stage 3 — Review and Clarification

Relevant information may be reviewed and clarification sought from appropriate parties.

#### Stage 4 — Resolution or Escalation

The School may provide clarification, correct information, implement technical changes, restrict inappropriate content, or refer the matter to authorised personnel.

#### Stage 5 — Communication

Where appropriate and lawful, the complainant may be informed of the outcome or next steps.

#### Stage 6 — Recordkeeping

Relevant complaint records may be retained according to the School’s approved procedures.

This process does not replace statutory complaint mechanisms or mandatory reporting obligations.

## 12. Urgent Safeguarding Complaints

Concerns involving suspected abuse, exploitation, grooming, serious harassment, or immediate threats to a child’s safety must not be treated as ordinary technical complaints. Such matters should be referred promptly to the Principal or the Designated Safeguarding Lead, as set out in the [Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy](/legal/child-safeguarding#s-4).

Where immediate danger exists, emergency services or appropriate authorities should be contacted without delay. The School shall follow applicable safeguarding and reporting requirements.

Nothing in this Policy requires a person to exhaust internal complaint procedures before contacting an authority where direct reporting is permitted or required by law.

## 13. Privacy and Personal Data Complaints

Complaints concerning personal information may include:

- Unauthorised disclosure.
- Incorrect personal records.
- Inappropriate access.
- Suspected data breaches.
- Unauthorised processing.
- Concerns about third-party services.
- Requests relating to applicable data protection rights.

Such complaints shall be referred to appropriately authorised personnel. The responsible legal entity shall comply with applicable statutory procedures and deadlines.

Users retain any applicable right to approach the relevant Malaysian regulatory authority.

## 14. Escalation of Complaints

Certain matters may require escalation beyond ordinary Platform administration. Examples include:

- Serious safeguarding concerns.
- Significant personal data incidents.
- Allegations involving senior school personnel.
- Material governance issues.
- Matters requiring legal advice.
- Concerns involving conflicts of interest.

Where a complaint concerns the Principal, the matter should be referred to the Chair of the Board of Governors of Sekolah Rendah Sathya Berhad or another suitable independent channel.

The person who is the subject of a complaint should not exercise sole control over its investigation or resolution.

## 15. Digital Governance Principles

The Platform shall be administered according to principles of responsible institutional governance. These include:

### 15.1 Defined Responsibilities

Operational, technical, educational, and governance responsibilities should be appropriately distinguished.

### 15.2 Authorised Access

Users should receive access only to information and functions necessary for their legitimate responsibilities.

### 15.3 Accountability

Significant administrative actions should be documented where appropriate.

### 15.4 Information Protection

Personal and confidential information must be handled according to applicable legal and institutional requirements.

### 15.5 Child-Centred Decision-Making

The welfare and best interests of pupils should remain central to decisions affecting child-facing services.

### 15.6 Continuous Improvement

The Platform may be reviewed and enhanced as the School’s digitalisation programme develops.

## 16. Administrative Roles and Permissions

The Platform may provide differentiated access for:

- Pupils.
- Parents and guardians.
- Teachers.
- Administrative employees.
- School leadership.
- Authorised governing representatives.
- Technical administrators.
- Other approved users.

Permissions should be assigned according to legitimate institutional responsibilities. Access to sensitive pupil records must not be granted solely because an individual holds a senior organisational or technical position.

Administrative privileges should be reviewed when duties change or institutional involvement ends.

## 17. Governance of Technical Development

SSIOM may support the development and maintenance of the Platform through authorised personnel or contributors. Technical changes should be coordinated with the School where they affect educational operations, pupil information, user access, or institutional procedures.

The School should assess the implications of significant new functions before deployment. Such assessments may consider:

- Educational suitability.
- Child safeguarding.
- Privacy and security.
- Operational readiness.
- Staff training.
- Accessibility.
- Data management.
- Third-party dependencies.

The School may delay or restrict the introduction of functions where necessary to address material risks.

## 18. Conflicts of Interest

Individuals involved in Platform governance, procurement, technical development, administration, or complaint handling should disclose relevant conflicts of interest where appropriate.

Decisions should be made in the legitimate interests of the School and its pupils. No individual should use Platform privileges to obtain improper personal advantage or unauthorised access to confidential information.

Where a conflict affects a complaint or decision, the matter should be referred to an appropriately independent authorised person.

## 19. Institutional Communications

Official Platform communications should be issued by authorised personnel. Users must not impersonate the School, SSIOM, the Board of Governors, or other institutional representatives.

Where the Platform supports notices, announcements, or other communications, appropriate publication permissions should be established. The School may correct or withdraw inaccurate or unauthorised communications.

## 20. Policy Review and Oversight

This Policy may be reviewed periodically and when significant changes occur in:

- Applicable legislation.
- School governance arrangements.
- Platform functionality.
- Accessibility requirements.
- Safeguarding practices.
- Complaint handling procedures.
- Technical infrastructure.

The Principal may coordinate operational reviews and refer matters requiring institutional approval to the appropriate governing authority.

## 21. Governing Law

This Policy shall be interpreted in accordance with applicable Malaysian law, including relevant education, child protection, personal data protection, and other legal requirements.

Nothing in this Policy prevents users from exercising statutory rights or seeking remedies through appropriate authorities.
