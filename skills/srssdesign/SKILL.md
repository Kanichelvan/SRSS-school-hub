# SRSS Design

Create SRSS digital experiences that feel calm, credible, distinctly Malaysian and rooted in values education. Preserve the approved visual hierarchy: disciplined navy brand surfaces, generous ivory space, restrained gold, authentic school imagery and energetic colour only inside the appropriate hub modules.

## Load the right references

Read [references/brand-system.md](references/brand-system.md) for every task.

Then read only what the requested surface needs:

- Public homepage, admissions, about, school-life or contact pages: read [references/public-site.md](references/public-site.md).

## Classify the surface first

Choose one mode before designing:

1. **Public brand surface** — homepage, about, values, learning, admissions, school life, news, contact. Use editorial restraint, solid navy and warm light surfaces. Do not use expressive gradients.
2. **Operational hub surface** — parent and teacher tools, notices, forms, calendars, resources. Use structured navy/white layouts with functional density and limited category accents.
3. **Student or content-gallery surface** — reading, activities, journals, challenges, achievements and galleries. Use the approved expressive gradients inside module headers and cards while keeping navigation and page structure calm.

Never mix all three modes in one viewport. Let the page structure establish calm; let individual modules provide energy only when their function warrants it.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Use the official school logo as a compact brand mark. Do not redraw, stylise or recreate it.
- Keep the logo confined to the header and, when appropriate, a restrained footer lockup. Never use it as a watermark, background texture or repeated motif.
- Do not use oversized SRSS emblems, anniversary art or flower motifs as homepage decoration.
- Do not add unrelated crosses, yin-yang marks, Chinese or Tamil decorative characters, lotuses, religious symbols, invented badges or school-building line art. Multilingual text that exists inside the official logo is part of the logo and must remain unaltered.
- Use solid navy in the public header and hero. Do not place gradients on the public homepage.
- Reserve bright gradients for internal galleries, student content and deliberate feature-module headers.
- Use Playfair Display for editorial headings, Poppins for UI labels and controls, and Open Sans for body copy.
- Use fictional pupils only when suitable real photography is unavailable. Match the real SRSS lavender-and-aubergine uniform and never present generated pupils as real students.
- Use image-level background blending for editorial photography. Avoid pasted rectangular stock photos, hard cut-outs, halos and CSS gradient overlays.
- Prefer spacing, type, alignment and fine rules over card grids, heavy borders, deep shadows and pill-shaped UI.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the supplied page, code, image, copy and brand assets.
2. If the user supplies photos, preserve the originals and run the intake, manifest and derivative workflow in `photo-intake-and-editing.md` before placing or editing them.
3. Classify the surface as public, operational hub or student/gallery.
4. Load the relevant references above.
5. Reuse the authentic assets. Generate only missing photography or illustrations.
6. Establish the navy/ivory/gold foundation and typography before adding category colour.
7. Design one clear hero or primary task. Remove secondary modules until the first viewport breathes.
8. Make navigation, search, menus, admissions/visit actions and visible forms work when building a prototype.
9. Verify desktop and mobile layouts, keyboard focus, contrast, image crops, uniform accuracy, console health and horizontal overflow.
10. Compare the implementation against the approved visual target. Fix headline wrapping, vertical rhythm, image integration and header alignment before handoff.

## Completion checklist

- School logo is official, small and used sparingly.
- Uploaded photo originals are preserved; processed derivatives and factual alt text are recorded in a manifest.
- Public surfaces contain no decorative gradients or unrelated symbols.
- Headings, UI labels and body copy use their assigned type roles.
- Navy, ivory and gold dominate; purple is a restrained label/accent on public pages.
- Student imagery is sharp, natural, multiethnic Malaysian and uniform-accurate.
- Photography blends into its surface without a visible pasted edge.
- The first viewport has one dominant message and no dashboard-like clutter.
- Hub colour is purposeful and constrained to modules.
- Mobile navigation and primary actions remain usable.
- Copy reflects human excellence, character and the five human values without sounding generic or overly devotional.
